                                     ABSTRACT

     This study investigates the relationship between financial management, challenges,
and coping mechanisms in the context of school management among private high
schools in Sanchez Mira, Cagayan. It reveals significant variations in school profiles,
with enrollments ranging from 190 to 599 students and teaching staff numbers
fluctuating between 8 and 18. While financial management practices are generally rated
as “Very Satisfactory,” particularly in fund allocation and expenditure, the reliance on
tuition for revenue generation highlights a critical need for additional income sources.
The financial challenges faced by these schools are primarily due to unstable family
incomes, leading to delayed tuition payments and reduced collection rates, particularly
in rural areas with lower enrollment. In response, schools have adopted coping strategies
such as flexible payment plans and enhanced fundraising efforts, although these require
ongoing refinement for sustained effectiveness. The study further identifies effective
leadership, staff performance, and curriculum alignment as strengths that support
student success and teacher satisfaction. A positive relationship between financial and
school management levels suggests that improved revenue generation can enhance
overall school performance. The conclusions emphasize the importance of diversifying

                                    INTRODUCTION

     For private schools to be sustainable and successful, they must practice effective
financial management, particularly in rural and semi-urban areas like Sanchez Mira
in the northern Philippines. Private schools are important parts of the local education
system in many areas and frequently the main or only source of educational possibilities
for many pupils (Daviet, 2023). These institutions’ ability to maintain facilities, support
their staff, and provide high-quality instruction depends critically on their financial
stability (Nkedishu and Onyekwe, 2024).
     Numerous financial difficulties that private schools in Sanchez Mira must contend
with can have a big influence on how they run and how well their students do in school.
Variations in enrolment rates are a significant problem since they can result in erratic
revenue sources. Because of this volatility, budgeting and financial planning become more
challenging, and schools find it challenging to uphold uniform operational standards.
Furthermore, a lot of the private schools in this region have little chance of receiving
government or outside assistance, making tuition fees their main source of income.
Because of their reliance on a single source of income, they are especially susceptible to
shifts in the local community’s financial situation and economic downturns. Increasing
operating expenses make these problems even worse. The rising cost of salaries, utilities,
upkeep, and instructional materials is something that schools must deal with and puts
further pressure on their finances. These financial strains are exacerbated in Sanchez
Mira, where there is a greater prevalence of economic instability and lower income
levels, which has an impact on the schools’ capacity to efficiently manage their resources.
     Various coping mechanisms are employed by private schools to overcome these
financial issues. Changing sources of income is one popular strategy (Yizengaw and
Agegnehu, 2021). Schools can hold fundraising events, ask for donations from past
students and nearby companies, or create new sources of income like school-based
businesses (Bhati and Hansen, 2020). These tactics act as a safety net against monetary
swings and augment tuition revenue. Another crucial tactic is efficient cost control. To

                                      FRAMEWORK

     Effective financial management is critical to the overall success and stability of private
schools. This framework integrates theoretical perspectives on financial management,
such as allocation, expenditure, and revenue sources—interact with the challenges
faced and coping strategies employed by private schools, ultimately impacting school
management.
     To preserve financial stability, financial management theory emphasizes the
significance of effectively allocating resources, controlling expenses, and diversifying
revenue streams. According to this view, efficient money management entails: Allocation
of resources to support institutional aims and operational demands, the budget is
strategically distributed across several areas (e.g., educational programs, facilities
maintenance, and administrative costs), spending control is the process of keeping an
eye on and reining in expenses to make sure money is spent wisely and to avoid going
overboard, and revenue diversification is the process of looking for sources of income
other than tuition (such as grants, gifts, and fundraisers) in order to lower risk and
improve resilience.

                                    METHODOLOGY

Research Design
     Mixed method sequential explanatory research design was used in the conduct of
the study. This design involves integrating qualitative and quantitative approaches to
gather a comprehensive understanding of the research questions.
     Quantitative part of the study focused on the profile of the school, level of financial
management and level of school management. Qualitative section covered the challenges
faced by school heads in managing resources as well as the coping mechanisms or
strategies employed to address identified challenges.

Locale of the Study
     The study was conducted at Sanchez Mira, Cagayan. Sanchez Mira is situated
along the coast of Luzon, bordered by the West Philippine Sea to the west and the
rugged terrain of the Sierra Madre Mountain range to the east. Its geographic location
lends it a unique blend of coastal and mountainous landscapes, offering residents and
students alike opportunities for both marine and terrestrial exploration. Despite its
relatively small size, Sanchez Mira boasts a vibrant educational landscape comprising
both public and private institutions. Private high schools in Sanchez Mira are integral
components of the local educational ecosystem, catering to a diverse student population
from various socio-economic backgrounds. These schools often emphasize values-based
education, academic rigor, and extracurricular enrichment, catering to the preferences
and aspirations of families seeking personalized learning experiences for their children.

Respondents and Sampling Procedure
     Respondents of the study are the different school heads in every private high school.
Complete enumeration was used to gather quantitative data pertaining to the profile of
the schools.
     On the qualitative part, purposive sampling was used to identify the respondents
who will be participating in interviews to collect pertinent information with regards
to the challenges faced by school heads in managing resources as well as the coping
strategies to address such. The following criteria will be used to determine potential
participants in the interview to highlight the value of the chosen technique: a) Must be
a school head for more than 3 years in an elementary school, b) must be assigned to the
designated locale of the study, and c) must be at least 40 years old.

Research Instrument
     There were two types of instruments which were used to gather pertinent data for
the study. The first instrument is a researcher made questionnaire that aims to gather
information about the profile of the school, level of financial management and level
of school management. This targets the quantitative part of the study. It underwent
validity and reliability test using Cronbach alpha.

Cronbach Alpha Result
     The reliability of the questionnaire was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. For
Financial Management (14 items), the alpha value was 0.84121, indicating good
internal consistency. For School Management (20 items), the alpha value was 0.81413,
also rated as good. These results confirm that the questionnaire is reliable and suitable
for evaluating both domains.
     For the qualitative part, the second instrument is an interview guide. Three basic
questions guided the researcher here: 1) What do you perceive as significant financial
challenges faced by your school? 2) How do these financial challenges impact the daily
operations of your school? 3) What strategies does your school currently use to manage
financial challenges? 4) How effective have these strategies been in maintaining financial
stability?

Data Gathering Procedure
     A formal request letter seeking permission to conduct the study was submitted to the
Department of Education’s schools’ division superintendent and the District Supervisor.
Upon receiving confirmation, another letter was sent directly to the principals of the
chosen research participants. This correspondence was forwarded by the researcher.
Subsequently, the informed consent form, which outlines the key aspects of the research
study and the implications of participation, was distributed to the study participants by
the researcher. Informed consent entails informing potential research participants about
the significant aspects of the study and the consequences of their involvement.

     Quantitative data was collected via questionnaire. Qualitative data collection was
conducted using online modalities and a questionnaire, with online interviews being
one of the primary methods employed. These interviews were facilitated through various
online platforms, video communications, and other forms of data synchronously. The
research protocol for conducting online interviews was encompass three distinct phases.
     In the initial stage of the study, the researcher provided a detailed explanation of the
study’s objectives and procedures to individuals who expressed interest in participating.
Ethical considerations were thoroughly discussed, emphasizing that participation in the
study is entirely voluntary. Participants were assured of anonymity, confidentiality, and
the freedom to withdraw from the study at any point. They were informed that any
potential inconveniences associated with the research process would be minimal, and
they were encouraged to discontinue their participation if they found it burdensome.
Upon their consent, participants received the interview guide containing the questions
via Messenger or Gmail. Additionally, participants were assured that any information
gathered during the interview would be treated with the utmost confidentiality and
used solely for educational purposes.
     In the second phase, the researcher coordinated a mutually convenient time for
both parties to conduct focus group discussion. This method was chosen to delve deeper
into participants’ responses, particularly those requiring further elaboration or probing.
Participants prompted to provide explanations based on their initial comments,
enabling the collection of additional information. Subsequently, all gathered data
compiled into a comprehensive master transcript by the researcher, facilitating analysis
and interpretation.
     In the third phase, the researcher arranged for additional interviews with certain
individuals after the data has been processed and analyzed. The objective of these
interviews was to validate the researcher’s interpretation of the theory developed from
the data. Participants were provided with a copy of the results and asked to confirm
whether they agreed with the fundamental structure of statements encapsulating their
experiences. Their feedback was considered during the final stages of data analysis and
presentation.

                                      CONCLUSION

Based on the findings, the conclusions were drawn:
     The study concludes that private high schools face unique financial and operational
challenges based on size, location, and available resources. Schools with larger
enrollments and more staff have higher budget needs, emphasizing the importance of
well-planned budgeting.
     Financial practices are generally effective, with good management of funds, but
there is a heavy reliance on tuition fees, suggesting schools should explore other revenue
sources like partnerships and fundraising to maintain stability.
     Financial issues, including delayed payments and lower enrollment, are common,
especially in rural areas. Schools are addressing these issues through flexible payment
plans, fundraising, and strong financial oversight, though these strategies need regular
updates to remain effective.
     Leadership, staff development, and curriculum alignment are strengths that
support both student success and teacher satisfaction. The study highlights that schools
with sound financial resources can better invest in staff development and management
practices, which in turn improve overall school performance.
     In essence, diversifying income, managing finances proactively, and building strong
leadership structures are key to sustaining high-quality private high schools and ensuring
long-term success in both teaching and student outcomes.

                                    RECOMMENDATIONS

    Based on the conclusions, here are five recommendations for enhancing financial
and operational effectiveness in private high schools:
    1. Schools should seek to expand income streams beyond tuition, including
        partnerships with local businesses, alumni fundraising, and grants. This
        diversification can help stabilize finances, especially during periods of low
        enrollment or delayed tuition payments.
    2. Regular financial reviews, along with strategic budgeting, are essential for
        managing costs related to teaching staff and operational needs. Schools with


212

                                                                   Peer Reviewed Journal


        larger enrollments or staff should implement more robust budgeting practices
        to support sustainable growth.
     3. Offering flexible payment plans can help families manage tuition payments
        more effectively, improving collection rates and reducing end-of-year deficits.
        Schools should consider promoting early payment discounts or providing
        additional incentives for timely payments.
     4. Schools should continue developing leadership skills among administrators
        and teaching staff. Training in governance, team management, and decision-
        making will improve school leadership quality, which is linked to better staff
        performance and student outcomes.
     5. Schools should periodically review and update financial management and
        operational practices to adapt to changing economic conditions and community
        needs. This includes refining fundraising strategies, optimizing resource
        allocation, and continuously assessing the effectiveness of implemented coping
        strategies.
