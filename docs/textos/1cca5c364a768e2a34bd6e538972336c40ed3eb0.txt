            1 INTRODUCTION


                     Accounting education has always been considered a critical component of
            secondary school education as it prepares students for financial, analytical, and
            entrepreneurial aspects of modern economic life. According to Mizzi (2023), Accounting
            and related business subjects contribute to learners’ understanding of sustainable
            economic participation, while sustainability education scholars highlight the importance
            of sustaining subjects that promote economic and civic competence (Sposab &
            Rieckmann, 2024). In addition, Accounting is a fundamental discipline that provides the
            knowledge and skills necessary for careers in finance, business management, economics,
            and entrepreneurship (Nwosu & Mahlaule, 2026). However, in South Africa, the
            sustainability of Accounting education has been a subject of debate for various reasons.
                     Thaba-Nkadimene et al. (2024) report that in the Limpopo Province, some schools
            have abandoned the subject due to low learner demand and other challenges. In addition,
            some learners report a lack of foundational knowledge in Economic and Management
            Sciences (EMS) and hold negative perceptions of the Accounting subject, which may
            explain their reluctance to study it in higher grades (Gobingca & Kiva, 2024). These

factors have contributed to a decline in the number of learners taking up Accounting, a
concern, especially in rural schools.
       According to Nwosu and Mahlaule (2026), the sustainability of Accounting
education is a concern because a decline in demand may affect the future supply of
accounting professionals. In addition to the challenges mentioned above, the subject faces
shortages of qualified teachers, ineffective teaching strategies, and a lack of support
systems for learners (Gobingca & Kiva, 2024; Thaba-Nkadimene et al., 2024). These
issues are more severe in rural areas, which have limited resources compared to urban
centers. While most of the literature discusses the learners’ and teachers’ perspectives on
Accounting sustainability, there is a need to understand the role of school management in
the subject’s sustainability.
       Heenan et al. (2023) argue that school management plays a critical role in school
improvement processes by influencing school culture and staff motivation. In addition,
transformational leadership has been found to positively affect teacher commitment and
organizational effectiveness (Alzoraiki et al., 2023). Transformational leaders have a
significant impact on their subordinates' performance, which is essential for promoting
educational sustainability (Karanxha, 2022). In addition, transformational leadership
influences other aspects of school organization, including staffing, budgeting, and
stakeholder relations (Heenan et al., 2023). Therefore, this study hypothesizes that school
management can promote the sustainability of Accounting education by influencing these
factors.
       Transformational Leadership Theory can be applied in this study because
transformational leaders can mobilize resources and motivate staff and stakeholders to
achieve organizational goals (Karanxha, 2022; Heenan et al., 2023). Transformational
leadership is considered the most effective leadership style for achieving educational
sustainability (Alzoraiki et al., 2023). Transformational leaders influence stakeholders
through motivation and support, which are critical to promoting the sustainability of
Accounting education. For instance, transformational leaders can motivate teachers
through effective communication, which is essential for addressing the challenges facing
Accounting education. In addition, transformational leadership can promote collaboration
among stakeholders, which is critical to ensuring the subject's sustainability.

                     While the literature indicates the role of school management in educational
            sustainability, there is limited information on how they can promote the sustainability of
            Accounting education. In addition, most of the discussion focuses on the perspectives of
            teachers and learners. Therefore, this study aims to determine the role of school
            management in promoting the sustainability of Accounting education. The study will be
            conducted in the Vhembe District of the Limpopo Province. Previous studies have shown
            that Accounting education has been declining in this district, with some schools
            abandoning the subject (Thaba-Nkadimene et al., 2024). Therefore, there is a need to
            understand how school management can address this issue, especially in rural secondary
            schools. The study was guided by the following research questions:
                  1. What school management practices contribute to the sustainability of Accounting
                     education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe District?
                  2. How do principals, Heads of Department, and Accounting teachers perceive the
                     role of school management in sustaining Accounting education?
                  3. What school management challenges hinder the sustainability of Accounting
                     education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe District?
                  4. What leadership strategies can be employed to strengthen the sustainability of
                     Accounting education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe District?


            2 LITERATURE REVIEW


                     The sustainability of Accounting education has been a subject of great interest as
            education systems worldwide seek to address the challenges of low enrolment, limited
            resources, and teacher shortages. Mizzi (2023) explains that business-related studies,
            including Accounting contribute to the creation of economically literate citizens, while
            Sposab and Rieckmann (2024) note that educational programs that foster economic
            literacy are critical in ensuring sustainable education. Accounting education, despite its
            benefits, is not always sustainable, particularly in secondary schools and in the developing
            world (Nwosu & Mahlaule, 2026).
                     Prior literature has shown that the sustainability of Accounting education is
            frequently influenced by a combination of student, teacher, and curriculum factors. For
            example, low enrolment numbers, insufficient foundational knowledge, and negative

perceptions of the subject have all been identified as critical student factors contributing
towards the unsustainability of Accounting education (Gobingca & Kiva, 2024).
Similarly, poor teacher performance and inadequate pedagogical skills have been shown
to discourage learners from pursuing the subject, thus impacting its sustainability (Nwosu
& Mahlaule, 2026). Nevertheless, such factors do not fully explain the mechanisms by
which Accounting education is sustained or not.
       This review argues that the sustainability of Accounting education should be
conceptualized as an administrative issue. Using Transformational Leadership Theory,
this review attempts to bridge the research gap by examining the relationship among
school management practices, stakeholders’ perceptions and challenges, and leadership
strategies to ensure the sustainability of school subjects such as Accounting.


2.1 Transformational leadership theory and accounting education sustainability


       This study is embedded in the Transformational Leadership Theory to explore the
factors that influence the sustainability of Accounting education. Transformational
leadership concerns leaders’ ability to influence followers to achieve collective goals
(Karanxha, 2022). Transformational leaders motivate, encourage, and inspire students to
achieve individual and organizational goals. Karanxha (2022) states that transformational
leadership is one of the most influential theories in education because it prioritizes
collaboration, commitment, creativity, and responsibility over transactional relationships.
Heenan et al. (2023) note that transformational leadership enhances school culture while
encouraging teacher commitment and performance.
       Transformational leadership has long been recognized as a critical leadership style
for attaining school and organizational effectiveness (Alzoraiki et al., 2023).
Transformational leaders inspire organizational members to achieve organizational goals
while creating a positive school culture that enhances performance. Transformational
leadership has been linked to improved student performance as well as teacher
commitment and engagement (Alzoraiki et al., 2023). However, the extent to which
transformational leadership influences the sustainability of subjects such as Accounting
remains poorly understood.

                     While Transformational Leadership Theory has been applied in education to
            explain school effectiveness, little is known about its role in ensuring the sustainability
            of educational programs and subjects such as Accounting. School management literature
            shows that transformational leadership is strongly associated with improved teacher
            performance, as well as the school’s resource base, culture, and environment (Heenan et
            al., 2023). Nevertheless, Alzoraiki et al. (2023) note that transformational leadership
            promotes sustainable teaching performance by encouraging teachers’ commitment,
            despite there being no information on whether such commitment influences the
            sustainability of school subjects.


            2.2 School management practices and accounting education sustainability


                     A review of the literature shows that the sustainability of Accounting education
            can be determined by the management practices that influence teachers and learners.
            Although prior research in accounting education has focused on student and teacher
            factors, the role of school management should not be overlooked. For example, Gobingca
            and Kiva (2024) link the poor enrolment of Accounting to learners’ poor foundational
            knowledge and preparation in Economic and Management Sciences. While such factors
            may explain poor enrolment of Accounting, they fail to explain why some schools with
            similar problems continue to offer the subject.
                     Similarly, although Nwosu and Mahlaule (2026) associate the unsustainability of
            Accounting education with teacher-related issues, including teacher shortages and poor
            teaching methods, these issues can be exacerbated by poor school management. The role
            of school leadership in enhancing the sustainability of Accounting education has been
            poorly understood. For example, educational leadership researchers have shown that
            transformational leadership fosters positive school culture and encourages teacher
            collaboration and commitment, which are critical for improving learning (Heenan et al.,
            2023). Such evidence suggests that the role of school management and leadership should
            be considered in discussions of the sustainability of Accounting education.

2.3 Stakeholders’ perceptions of school management in sustaining accounting
education


       The perceptions of stakeholders such as teachers, Heads of Department, and
principals are critical considerations when discussing the role of leadership in education.
While the impact of leadership on organizational effectiveness has been widely
documented, the focus has been on outcomes rather than on organizational members'
perceptions of leadership. For example, Heenan et al. (2023) note that positive
perceptions of transformational leadership motivate stakeholders and improve
organizational performance. Similarly, Thaba-Nkadimene et al. (2024) argue that
stakeholder perceptions are key to achieving organizational sustainability. If teachers
have positive perceptions of transformational leadership, for example, they are more
likely to be motivated, thereby fostering the sustainability of Accounting education.
       An important consideration when discussing the role of leadership is the extent to
which school managers understand the needs of the school community. In other words,
what stakeholders perceive as school leadership's intentions may differ markedly from
what leaders think. According to Thaba-Nkadimene et al. (2024), poor communication
and consultation between stakeholders have been identified as key factors that lead to the
discontinuation of Accounting in some schools. Their findings show that poor stakeholder
engagement may affect the sustainability of Accounting education. From a
transformational leadership perspective, the ability of leaders to listen to the needs of the
community is critical in fostering positive perceptions of leadership among organizational
members (Karanxha, 2022).


2.4 Challenges constraining accounting education sustainability


       Literature on Accounting education sustainability describes a set of institutional,
teacher, student, and leadership factors that influence the continuous provision of
Accounting education. Although researchers often identify various challenges, there is
often insufficient evidence on the factors that account for most accounting education
discontinuities. Gobingca & Kiva (2024) note that learners’ lack of interest in studying
Accounting is often linked to their perceptions of the subject and their inadequate

            2.5 Leadership strategies that can be used to sustain accounting education


                     Most researchers who have studied the factors affecting the sustainability of
            accounting education do not discuss the best strategies for addressing the problem. Those
            who discuss solutions often focus on either student, teacher, or institutional factors. For
            example, Gobingca & Kiva (2024) recommend improving learner preparation and
            providing foundational knowledge to address poor enrolment in Accounting. Similarly,
            Nwosu & Mahlaule (2026) recommend that improving teacher performance and
            addressing teacher-related issues would improve the situation. However, such
            recommendations overlook the role of school leadership in influencing student and
            teacher performance. Transformational leadership research shows that effective leaders
            are those who motivate, encourage, and inspire organizational members to achieve
            organizational goals (Alzoraiki et al., 2023). As such, addressing both teacher and
            student-related issues requires transformational leadership that guides and encourages
            both groups.
                     Some studies such as Alzoraiki et al (2023) and Heenan et al. (2023) show that
            sustainable educational outcomes are achieved by leaders who encourage resource

2.6 Research gap


       The overall sentiment of the reviewed literature is that the sustainability of
Accounting education is determined by student, teacher, institutional, and leadership
factors. Most researchers agree that negative perceptions of Accounting among learners,
as well as a lack of foundational knowledge and skills, contribute to its unsustainability
(Gobingca & Kiva, 2024). Similarly, most studies have shown that poor teacher
performance and pedagogical skills often lead to ineffective teaching and learning of
Accounting, further undermining its sustainability (Nwosu & Mahlaule, 2026).
       Researchers such as Thaba-Nkadimene et al. (2024) have shown that poor
communication and consultation among stakeholders often lead to discontinuation of
Accounting education, even when it is needed. The extant literature shows that little is
known about how school management influences the sustainability of Accounting
education. While most studies have examined the impact of student and teacher factors,
there is little information on how school leadership affects the situation. Transformational
leadership theory has been widely used in education to improve teacher performance and,
by extension, student performance (Alzoraiki et al., 2023). Transformational leaders
inspire organizational members to achieve collective goals through motivation,
encouragement, and inspiration (Karanxha, 2022).
       However, as Alzoraiki et al. (2023) note, transformational leadership is often
focused on achieving organizational effectiveness and sustainability by optimizing
resources. The extent to which transformational leadership influences the sustainability
of school subjects, such as Accounting, is poorly understood, particularly in public
secondary schools. As such, this study aims to contribute to the body of literature that
explores the role of school management in sustaining Accounting education.

            3 METHODS AND MATERIALS


                     The study adopted a descriptive qualitative multiple-case study design to
            investigate the role of school management in promoting Accounting education in public
            secondary schools in the Vhembe District of the Limpopo Province. A qualitative
            approach was used because the study focused on understanding participants’ views and
            leadership roles in promoting Accounting education in its natural setting (Creswell &
            Poth, 2018; Yin, 2018). Multiple cases were used to compare the role of school
            management across different institutions offering Accounting.
                     The study was conducted in Vhembe District, Limpopo Province, South Africa,
            which has been reported to have declining numbers of students enrolled in Accounting
            and the discontinuation of the subject in some schools (Thaba-Nkadimene et al., 2024).
            Purposive sampling was used to select eight (8) public secondary schools in Thaba-
            Njadimene because of the respondents’ in-depth knowledge of the phenomenon under
            study (Patton, 2015). The sample size comprised 24 participants:
                  • Eight (8) Principals,
                  • Eight (8) Heads of Department (Commerce),
                  • Eight (8) Accounting Teachers.
                     Data saturation was achieved after about twenty (20) interviews when no new
            information or themes emerged. Four (4) more interviews were conducted to confirm
            saturation, making a total of twenty-four (24) interviewees (Guest et al., 2020).
                     Semi-structured interviews were conducted to allow participants to provide in-
            depth responses to the research questions while giving the researcher control over the
            information gathered. The questions were developed from the research problem and
            objectives and informed by Transformational Leadership Theory. The protocol followed
            included asking open-ended questions on participants’ views and leadership roles in
            promoting Accounting education, exploring themes related to the phenomenon, and
            reviewing issues raised by participants. The interviews were conducted face-to-face,
            lasted 45 to 60 minutes, and were tape-recorded with the participants’ consent. They were
            later transcribed verbatim for analysis.
                     The thematic analysis of interview data was conducted using Braun and Clarke’s
            (2006) six steps: familiarising oneself with the data, generating initial codes, developing

themes, reviewing themes, defining themes, and reporting findings. The transcripts were
initially coded deductively using concepts and definitions related to Transformational
Leadership Theory. Later, additional codes were generated inductively to capture
information specific to the study’s phenomenon. Similar approaches were used in
studying the phenomenon related to Accounting education sustainability (Gobingca &
Kiva, 2024; Thaba-Nkadimene et al., 2024).
       The study followed guidelines for ensuring trustworthiness, including credibility,
transferability, dependability, and confirmability (Lincoln & Guba, 1985). Credibility
was achieved by allowing participants to review verbatim transcripts and approve the
interpretation of the findings. Transferability was enhanced by providing detailed
information about the study’s respondents and setting, enabling readers to assess the
applicability of the findings. Dependability was maintained by keeping a reflective
journal throughout the research process to assess the extent of consistency between the
process and the findings. Lastly, confirmability was achieved by using verbatim quotes
to support the findings.
       The study was approved by the university’s ethics committee. The permission to
undertake the research was granted by the Limpopo Department of Education and the
respective schools. In addition, the researcher obtained informed consent from the
participants before conducting interviews. The participants’ anonymity, confidentiality,
and right to withdraw from the study at any time were guaranteed.


4 FINDINGS


       This section presents the research results on the influence of school management
on the sustainability of Accounting education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe
district, Limpopo province. The results are presented under the following themes that
emerged from the thematic analysis of interviews with the selected participants: school
leadership practices promoting the sustainability of Accounting education, stakeholders’
perceptions of school management, challenges to the continuation of Accounting
education, and school leadership skills for promoting subject sustainability. In general,
the results demonstrate that school management plays a key role in facilitating or
hindering the provision of Accounting education in rural secondary schools.

            4.1 Theme 1: leadership practices as enablers of accounting education sustainability


                     The findings revealed that school management plays a critical role in enhancing
            Accounting education sustainability. The participants reported that Accounting education
            sustainability is achieved through the leadership practices of the school management in
            ensuring that learners enrol for the learning of Accounting. The participants expounded
            that the management of the schools encourages the Accounting teachers to develop
            strategies for sustaining Accounting education because of the commitment demonstrated
            in promoting the subject. The participants explained that the management encourages
            learners to study Accounting because it has lucrative career opportunities. It was also
            reported that the management is involved in monitoring the performance of the
            Accounting teachers and providing the necessary support to them in case of any form of
            challenge affecting the teaching of Accounting.
                     “The Principal ensures that Accounting is treated equal to other subjects such as
            Mathematics and Science. The management monitors the performance of Accounting
            teachers and ensures that they are provided with the necessary support in case any form
            of a challenge arises.”(Principal P1)
                     “The school management encourages us to participate in workshops and other
            related training programs because they believe that their continuous education
            contributes to the quality of the subject”(Accounting Teacher AT3)
                     “The Principal always encourages learners in assemblies to study Accounting
            because of the many career opportunities it offers.”(Head of Department HOD2)
                     “The school management is always ready to provide textbooks or any form of
            learning materials in case of any form of a challenge”(Accounting Teacher AT5)
                     This is an indication that the participants viewed school management’s leadership
            practices as enablers to Accounting education sustainability. The participants expounded
            that when the school management practices leadership by encouraging the Accounting
            teachers through supporting them, offering motivation and rewarding them for good
            performance increases the teachers’ motivation and subsequently enhances Accounting
            education sustainability.

4.2 Theme 2: stakeholders’ perception of school management regarding subject

perception of the value of the subject from the learners with the failure to maintain the
subject in the school curriculum. The participants revealed that the principal is the major
stakeholder responsible for ensuring that the school curriculum is well maintained

demonstrate a positive attitude towards the resolution of the issue affecting Accounting

            5 DISCUSSION


                     The study examined the role of school management in promoting Accounting
            education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe District of the Limpopo province.
            The Transformational Leadership Theory revealed that the maintenance of Accounting
            subjects was determined by a combination of factors, including leadership, stakeholder
            perceptions, institutional challenges, and strategies that promote the continuation of the
            subject. Contrary to the assumption that the persistence of Accounting subjects is
            dependent on the demand by students and teachers, the study revealed that school
            management plays a critical role in promoting or eliminating Accounting subjects in the
            schools. The study then related the findings to the existing literature in the field and
            discussed the similarities, differences, and relationships in detail.
                     The study established that instructional leadership, teacher support, resource
            mobilisation, and active promotion of Accounting were key areas of focus for sustaining
            Accounting education. This finding was consistent with the results of Heenan et al.
            (2023), who established that transformational leadership promoted positive school culture
            in which teachers were motivated and contributed to educational improvement. Similarly,
            Alzoraiki et al. (2023) also found that transformational leadership had a positive influence
            on sustainable teaching performance through enhanced teacher commitment and
            organisational support. Unlike previous research, which mainly focused on the role of
            leadership in education, the current study revealed how leadership facilitated the
            sustainability of a specific curricular area. This finding indicated the role of school
            management in creating conducive environments that promote vulnerable subjects. This

6 RECOMMENDATIONS


6.1 Recommendations for the South African education system


       The study recommends that the South African education system consider the
sustainability of Accounting by enhancing leadership capabilities. The latter should be
transformational so that schools can prioritize Accounting, support teachers continuously,
and monitor learners’ performance. The Department of Education should place more
emphasis on recruiting, training, and sustaining quality Accounting teachers, especially
in rural schools, and boost career guidance programs to help learners understand the
benefits of studying Accounting and develop positive attitudes towards the subject. It is
also recommended that connections be maintained at school, parent-teacher, business-
enterprise, professional bodies, higher education, and district levels to mobilize resources
to support Accounting education and create an effective framework for its sustainability.
In addition, districts should develop monitoring structures to reveal the status of the

            6.2 Recommendations for further research


                     Further studies can be conducted to assess the sustainability of Accounting across
            different districts and provinces, so that inferences drawn in the Vhembe District can be
            generalized across South Africa. The study can also be replicated across schools that have
            abandoned Accounting and compare their leadership styles and strategies with those of
            schools that have sustained it. Future researchers should consider using mixed-methods

            role of educational leadership and the sustainability of Accounting education. They
            should also attempt to include the views of other stakeholders, such as learners, parents,
            subject advisors, and district officials, in their studies. Lastly, the next researcher needs

            officials understand how the subject can be sustained in schools.


            7 CONTRIBUTION AND ORIGINALITY OF THE STUDY


                     This study makes an original contribution by repositioning Accounting education
            sustainability as a school leadership and management issue rather than merely a learner-,
            teacher-, or curriculum-related challenge. Grounded in Transformational Leadership
            Theory, the study extends existing scholarship by demonstrating how leadership practices
            such as instructional support, stakeholder engagement, resource mobilisation, and
            strategic advocacy influence the retention of Accounting in public secondary schools. The
            study further contributes context-specific empirical evidence from the under-researched
            Vhembe District of Limpopo Province, thereby enriching the literature on educational
            leadership, curriculum sustainability, and subject retention in rural schooling contexts.
            By highlighting school management as a critical determinant of Accounting education
            sustainability, the study provides a novel perspective that can inform policy, practice, and
            future research aimed at strengthening the long-term viability of Accounting education in
            South Africa.

8 CONCLUSION


       This study examined the role of school management in sustaining Accounting
education in public secondary schools in the Vhembe District of Limpopo Province
through the lens of Transformational Leadership Theory. The findings revealed that the
sustainability of Accounting education is strongly influenced by leadership practices such
as instructional support, resource mobilisation, stakeholder engagement, and the
promotion of Accounting as a viable career pathway. While challenges including
declining learner enrolment, teacher shortages, and resource constraints continue to
threaten the subject, the study demonstrated that proactive and transformational school
leadership can mitigate these challenges and strengthen subject retention. The study
therefore concludes that effective school management is a critical determinant of
Accounting education sustainability and that leadership-driven strategies are essential for
ensuring the continued viability of Accounting education in rural secondary school
contexts.
