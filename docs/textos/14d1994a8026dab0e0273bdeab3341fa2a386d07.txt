                                     tion, and potential risks in deployment of AI solutions. The rapid proliferation of AI
                                     technologies in higher education has created significant challenges for institutional
                                     leaders who must balance technological advancement with educational outcomes,
                                     ethical considerations, and resource constraints. This study addresses these critical
                                     challenges by developing a comprehensive taxonomy of AI applications in higher
                                     education leadership. Through a systematic literature review and inductive analysis

                                     higher education contexts, provides educational leaders with a structured framework
                                     for understanding, evaluating, and implementing AI solutions in their institutions.
                                     This study contributes to the field by offering a common language and conceptual
                                     framework for researchers, policymakers, and practitioners, while also identifying criti-
                                     cal areas for future research. The findings underscore the transformative potential of AI
                                     in higher education and the need for a balanced approach that leverages technologi-
                                     cal advancements while addressing ethical considerations and equity issues.

                   integration of AI in educational settings has the potential to revolutionize administra-
                   tive processes, enhance teaching and learning experiences, and reshape decision-making
                   paradigms (Chen et al., 2020; Popenici & Kerr, 2017). From automating routine tasks to
                   providing personalized learning experiences, AI is poised to fundamentally alter the way
                   educational institutions operate and deliver their core mission (Brusilovsky, 2024).
                     Current educational systems struggle with increasing demands for personalization,
                   efficiency, and accountability, while simultaneously facing resource constraints and
                   evolving technological landscapes (Zawacki-Richter et al., 2024). AI technologies offer
                   potential solutions to these challenges, yet their implementation in educational con-
                   texts remains fragmented and often poorly understood by leadership (Sposato, 2024).
                   The potential of AI in education extends far beyond mere automation (ElSayary, 2024).
                   It offers the promise of more adaptive and responsive educational systems, capable of
                   meeting the diverse needs of learners in ways previously unimaginable. For instance, AI-
                   powered adaptive learning platforms can tailor educational content and pacing to indi-
                   vidual student needs, potentially addressing long-standing challenges in differentiated
                   instruction (Tang et al., 2020). Similarly, AI-driven analytics can provide educational
                   leaders with deep insights into student performance trends, enabling more informed
                   and timely interventions (Jiao et al., 2022).
                     However, the multifaceted nature of AI applications in education, coupled with the
                   unique responsibilities of educational leaders, necessitates a comprehensive understand-
                   ing of how AI can be effectively leveraged in educational leadership (Mao et al., 2024).
                   This understanding is essential for optimizing institutional performance, addressing
                   ethical considerations and ensuring equitable access to AI-enhanced educational expe-
                   riences (Nguyen et al., 2022). Educational leaders must navigate a complex landscape
                   of technological possibilities while considering the broader implications of AI adoption
                   on issues such as privacy, data security, and the changing nature of work in educational
                   settings.
                     Despite the growing body of research on AI in education, there exists a notable gap
                   in the literature regarding a systematic categorization of AI applications specifically
                   tailored to educational leadership. This gap hinders the ability of educational leaders
                   to fully comprehend and strategically implement AI solutions in their institutions (Ng
                   et al., 2024). While numerous studies have explored specific applications of AI in edu-
                   cational contexts, there is a lack of a comprehensive framework that synthesizes these
                   diverse applications into a coherent taxonomy relevant to educational leadership.
                     To address this gap, this study aims to answer the following research question: How
                   can the applications of AI in educational leadership be systematically categorized to
                   provide a comprehensive framework for understanding and implementation? The sig-
                   nificant contribution of this article lies in the development of a comprehensive taxon-
                   omy of AI applications in educational leadership. This taxonomy serves as a structured
                   framework for understanding, evaluating, and implementing AI solutions in educational
                   institutions. By bridging the gap between theoretical advancements in AI and practical
                   applications in educational settings, this study provides a valuable resource for research-
                   ers, policymakers, and practitioners alike. The taxonomy categorizes existing AI applica-
                   tions and identifies potential areas for future development, thereby offering a roadmap
                   for the strategic integration of AI in educational leadership.

                   Literature review and conceptual framework
                   The landscape of AI in educational leadership has evolved significantly since 2017, char-
                   acterized by transformative developments in both technological capabilities and peda-
                   gogical applications (Sarrion, 2023). This evolution has been marked by several distinct
                   phases of development and implementation, each contributing to our current under-
                   standing of AI’s role in educational leadership. The foundational work of Popenici and
                   Kerr (2017) established critical concepts regarding AI’s potential in higher education,
                   particularly stressing the need for balanced integration of technology with pedagogical
                   principles. This early framework has been subsequently expanded and refined through
                   numerous empirical studies and theoretical developments.
                      Recent comprehensive reviews, particularly the work of Bond et al. (2024), have high-
                   lighted the increasing sophistication of AI applications in educational settings, while
                   also underlining growing concerns about ethical implementation and equity considera-
                   tions. Their meta-systematic review identified significant gaps in current research, par-
                   ticularly in the areas of ethical frameworks and collaborative implementation strategies.
                   This work has been complemented by Zawacki-Richter et al. (2024), who documented
                   the evolution of AI applications in higher education, noting the shift from simple auto-
                   mation tools to more complex, adaptive systems capable of supporting sophisticated
                   educational processes.
                      In the scope of administrative efficiency and operational management, several key
                   studies have documented the transformative potential of AI systems. Abayomi et al.
                   (2021) demonstrated significant improvements in library management systems through
                   AI implementation, while Kuleto et al. (2021) documented how AI-driven analytics have
                   enhanced institutional decision-making processes. These operational developments
                   have been particularly notable in resource allocation and scheduling optimization,
                   areas where AI has shown substantial promise in reducing administrative burden while
                   improving efficiency.
                      The personalized learning domain has seen particularly rapid development, with stud-
                   ies by Tang et al. (2020) documenting the effectiveness of adaptive learning systems in
                   meeting individual student needs. Their research demonstrated how AI-powered plat-
                   forms can successfully adjust content delivery and learning paths based on individual
                   student performance patterns. This work has been further expanded by more recent
                   studies, such as Essel et al. (2022), which showed how virtual teaching assistants can
                   effectively support student learning through personalized interactions and feedback.
                      Ethical considerations and governance frameworks have emerged as critical areas of
                   focus, particularly in recent years. The work of Nguyen et al. (2022) has been instru-
                   mental in establishing fundamental principles for ethical AI implementation in educa-
                   tional settings. Their research highlighted the need for comprehensive frameworks that
                   address issues of privacy, fairness, and transparency in AI deployment. This has been
                   complemented by studies examining the impact of AI on educational equity and access,
                   with Xia et al. (2022) providing important insights into both opportunities and chal-
                   lenges in this area.
                      The strategic planning and leadership aspects of AI implementation have been exam-
                   ined in detail by several researchers. Wang (2021) explored the symbiotic relationship
                   between human and AI in educational decision-making, proposing frameworks for

                   Methodology
                   A general inductive approach has been employed to analyze and synthesize existing lit-
                   erature on AI in higher education leadership (Thomas, 2006). The timeframe of 2017–
                   2024 was specifically chosen as 2017 marked a critical turning point with the publication
                   of seminal works establishing foundational concepts regarding AI’s potential in higher
                   education (Popenici & Kerr, 2017), while 2024 represents the current state of rapidly
                   evolving AI technologies in education (Bond et al., 2024).
                     The literature review phase involved a comprehensive and systematic search of peer-
                   reviewed articles, conference proceedings, and books. Multiple databases were strate-
                   gically selected based on their complementary strengths: IEEE Xplore for technical AI
                   implementations, ACM Digital Library for computing applications in education, ERIC
                   for educational research depth, and Scopus for broad academic coverage (Bell et al.,
                   2022). Primary search terms included variations of " AI in education," "AI leadership,"
                   "educational technology," "AI implementation in schools," and "educational administra-
                   tion AI." Secondary search terms encompassed "machine learning education," "auto-
                   mated educational systems," and "AI ethics education."
                     Publications were screened using specific inclusion criteria: (a) direct focus on AI
                   applications in higher education contexts, (b) relevance to leadership or administrative
                   aspects, (c) peer-reviewed sources, (d) publication within the selected timeframe, and
                   (e) English language publications. The initial search yielded 1,247 potentially relevant
                   papers, which through the application of these criteria was reduced to 314 publications
                   for detailed review.
                     The data analysis phase employed an iterative coding process using NVivo software.
                   Two independent researchers evaluated each publication’s methodological rigor, theo-
                   retical foundation, and relevance to the research questions. The taxonomy development
                   process involved systematic consolidation of these preliminary categories. Through iter-
                   ative analysis and comparison, patterns emerged that suggested natural clustering into
                   ten distinct domains.
                     These domains were validated through three rounds of review and refinement: initial
                   categorization based on functional similarities, validation against existing frameworks in

                   Domain categorization analysis and development
                   The development of the ten distinct domains presented in this study emerged through
                   a rigorous analytical process that combined both theoretical framework analysis
                   and empirical evidence examination. The initial identification of these domains was
                   grounded in a systematic analysis of implementation patterns and theoretical frame-
                   works documented in the literature from 2017 to 2024, with particular attention to how
                   different AI applications cluster around specific educational leadership functions.
                     The process began with a comprehensive examination of existing taxonomies and
                   frameworks, particularly those presented in the meta-systematic review by Bond et al.
                   (2024). This foundational analysis was enriched by incorporating insights from Zawacki-
                   Richter et al.’s (2024) work on new advances in AI applications in higher education. The
                   initial categorization process identified recurring patterns in AI implementation across
                   different educational contexts, which were then refined through multiple iterations of
                   analysis and validation.
                     The emergence of the ten distinct domains was further validated through the exami-
                   nation of practical implementation cases, as documented by Sposato (2024) and others.
                   Each domain was established based on both theoretical foundations and practical appli-
                   cations, ensuring that the categorization reflected both academic understanding and
                   real-world implementation needs. The domains were specifically designed to address the
                   full spectrum of educational leadership responsibilities, from operational management
                   to strategic planning and ethical governance.
                     The validation process for these domains involved multiple stages of refinement and
                   testing. The work of Brusilovsky (2024) on AI in education and human-AI collaboration
                   provided important insights into how these domains interact and overlap in practical
                   applications. This was complemented by an analysis of generative AI applications (Mao
                   et al., 2024), which helped ensure the taxonomy remained relevant to emerging tech-
                   nologies and applications.
                     The process of domain identification and refinement was particularly influenced by
                   the ethical considerations raised in recent literature. Nguyen et al.’s (2022) work on ethi-
                   cal principles for AI in education helped shape the inclusion of ethical considerations

                   Taxonomy of AI in educational leadership
                   A comprehensive taxonomy of AI applications in educational leadership has been devel-
                   oped through a systematic analysis of literature from 2017 to 2024. This timeframe was
                   specifically chosen to capture the transformative impact of recent AI advancements in
                   education, particularly following breakthrough developments in machine learning and
                   natural language processing (Zawacki-Richter et al., 2019). The emergence of large lan-
                   guage models and generative AI technologies that have fundamentally altered the edu-
                   cational landscape has been notably encompassed in this period (Lee & Palmer, 2025).
                      Through rigorous analysis of publications from multiple databases including IEEE
                   Xplore, ACM Digital Library, ERIC, and Scopus, ten distinct domains have been identi-
                   fied where AI technologies are making significant impacts or showing promising poten-
                   tial. The developed taxonomy categorizes these AI applications in educational leadership
                   into the following main domains:

                    1.    AI for Administrative Efficiency.
                    2.    AI for Personalized Learning.
                    3.    AI for Enhancing Teaching Practices.

                    5.    AI for Enhancing Student Support Services.
                    6.    AI in Organizational Leadership and Strategic Planning.
                    7.    AI for Governance and Compliance.
                    8.    AI for Community Engagement and Communication.
                    9.    Ethical AI Leadership and Governance.
                   10.    AI for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) Initiatives.

                     In the domain of Administrative Efficiency, AI applications have been implemented,
                   including automated scheduling systems that optimize class timetables and room assign-
                   ments (Abayomi et al., 2021), and data-driven decision support tools that analyze insti-
                   tutional data to provide insights on student performance and budget forecasting (Kuleto
                   et al., 2021). Within the Personalized Learning domain, adaptive learning platforms have
                   been developed using AI algorithms to adjust the difficulty and pace of educational con-
                   tent based on individual student performance and learning styles (Tang et al., 2020).
                   Intelligent tutoring systems have been designed to offer personalized feedback and sup-
                   port outside of classroom hours, serving as virtual tutors that can adapt to each student’s
                   needs (Essel et al., 2022).
                     The domain of Enhancing Teaching Practices has been strengthened through AI-
                   powered curriculum design tools that analyze student data and learning outcomes to
                   help educators and administrators refine curricula based on identified needs, gaps, and
                   performance trends (Bosman et al., 2023). Teacher professional development platforms
                   have been enhanced using AI to recommend targeted training opportunities based on
                   individual teacher performance and emerging educational trends (Chiu et al., 2022).
                     In the fied of Decision-Making and Policy Formulation, predictive analytics for pol-
                   icy development have been implemented, using AI algorithms to forecast the potential
                   outcomes of policy decisions (Wang, 2021). Ethical and equity decision support systems
                   have been developed using AI to ensure that decisions regarding student admission,
                   discipline, or resource allocation are free from biases, promoting fairness and equity in
                   educational institutions (Zhang et al., 2022).
                     The taxonomy’s development has been accomplished through a systematic three-stage
                   process, as outlined by Bond et al. (2024). The first stage involved constructing an initial
                   framework based on fundamental educational leadership functions. The second stage
                   refined this framework through detailed analysis of AI implementation cases in educa-
                   tional settings (Sposato, 2024), examining over 200 case studies worldwide. The third
                   stage validated the domains against emerging trends in AI education research (Brusi-
                   lovsky, 2024).
                     The taxonomy’s application has been designed to span multiple educational contexts,
                   including K-12 education, higher education, vocational training, and continuing educa-
                   tion. Each domain has been analyzed for its specific manifestations across these different
                   educational settings, acknowledging the unique challenges and opportunities present
                   in each context. The comprehensive nature of this taxonomy highlights the pervasive
                   potential of AI across various aspects of educational leadership, from streamlining
                   administrative tasks to fostering inclusive learning environments.


                   Domain analysis and applications
                   Administrative efficiency and operational management
                   The first domain, Administrative Efficiency, has emerged as a foundational area where
                   AI technologies are revolutionizing educational leadership. Recent implementations
                   have demonstrated significant developments in resource allocation and administrative
                   processes (Abayomi et al., 2021). Advanced scheduling systems utilizing machine learn-
                   ing algorithms have been shown to reduce administrative workload by up to 40% while
                   improving resource utilization (Kuleto et al., 2021).

                     Data-driven decision support systems within this domain have evolved to incorpo-
                   rate predictive analytics, enabling proactive management of institutional resources. For
                   instance, AI-powered enrollment management systems have demonstrated the ability to
                   predict student enrollment patterns with accuracy rates exceeding 85% (Jiao et al., 2022).
                   These systems integrate multiple data sources, including demographic trends, historical
                   enrollment data, and economic indicators, to provide comprehensive insights for insti-
                   tutional planning.


                   Personalized learning and educational delivery
                   The second domain focuses on AI applications in personalizing educational experiences.
                   Recent developments in adaptive learning platforms have shown promising results in
                   addressing individual student needs at scale. Studies have demonstrated that AI-driven
                   personalization can lead to significant improvements in student engagement and learn-
                   ing outcomes (Tang et al., 2020). These systems utilize sophisticated algorithms to ana-
                   lyze student performance patterns and adjust content delivery in real-time.
                     Intelligent tutoring systems within this domain have evolved to incorporate natural
                   language processing and cognitive modeling capabilities. Recent implementations have
                   shown particular success in supporting diverse learning styles and needs (Essel et al.,
                   2022). These systems continuously adapt to student responses and learning patterns,
                   providing personalized feedback and support that complements traditional instruction
                   methods.


                   Teaching enhancement and pedagogical innovation
                   The third domain encompasses AI applications that augment and enhance teaching
                   practices. Advanced curriculum design tools employing machine learning algorithms
                   have demonstrated effectiveness in identifying learning gaps and suggesting targeted
                   interventions (Bosman et al., 2023). These tools analyze vast amounts of student perfor-
                   mance data to identify patterns and trends that might be overlooked in traditional cur-
                   riculum development processes.
                     Professional development platforms in this domain have evolved to provide personal-
                   ized recommendations for teacher training and support. Recent implementations have
                   shown success in matching teachers with relevant professional development opportuni-
                   ties based on their teaching patterns and student outcomes (Chiu et al., 2022). These sys-
                   tems utilize natural language processing to analyze classroom interactions and provide
                   actionable feedback for teaching improvement.


                   Decision‑Making and policy development
                   The fourth domain focuses on AI applications in educational policy formulation and
                   decision-making processes. Advanced predictive analytics tools have been developed to
                   forecast the potential impacts of policy changes on student outcomes and institutional
                   performance (Wang, 2021). These systems integrate multiple data sources to provide
                   comprehensive policy impact assessments.
                     Recent implementations have shown particular success in identifying potential equity

                   Student support services and well‑being
                   The fifth domain addresses the critical area of student support services. AI-based career
                   counseling systems have demonstrated effectiveness in providing personalized guidance
                   based on individual student interests and abilities (Westman et al., 2021). These systems
                   analyze various factors, including academic performance, extracurricular activities, and
                   career trends, to provide tailored recommendations. Mental health and behavioral ana-
                   lytics systems within this domain have evolved to incorporate sophisticated pattern rec-
                   ognition algorithms. Recent implementations have shown promise in early identification
                   of students at risk of academic or personal difficulties (Essel et al., 2022).


                   Strategic planning and organizational leadership
                   The sixth domain focuses on AI applications in strategic planning and organizational
                   leadership. Advanced resource allocation systems have demonstrated effectiveness in
                   optimizing institutional resources while maintaining educational quality (Chiu et al.,
                   2023). These systems utilize machine learning algorithms to analyze complex patterns in
                   resource utilization and institutional performance.
                     Risk management and crisis response platforms in this domain have evolved to incor-
                   porate predictive analytics for identifying potential institutional challenges. Recent
                   implementations have shown success in helping institutions prepare for and respond to
                   various scenarios, from enrollment fluctuations to emergency situations (Fullan et al.,
                   2024).


                   Governance and compliance management
                   The seventh domain addresses the critical area of educational governance and compli-
                   ance. AI-powered compliance monitoring systems have demonstrated effectiveness in
                   ensuring adherence to educational standards and regulations (Kaur et al., 2022). These
                   systems utilize natural language processing and pattern recognition to analyze institu-
                   tional data and identify potential compliance issues.


                   Community engagement and communication
                   The eighth domain focuses on AI applications in fostering community relationships and
                   communication. Advanced communication platforms have shown success in improving
                   engagement with various stakeholders (Wang et al., 2023). These systems utilize natural
                   language processing and sentiment analysis to ensure effective and appropriate commu-
                   nication across diverse audiences.


                   Ethical AI leadership and governance
                   The ninth domain addresses the critical area of ethical AI implementation and govern-
                   ance. Recent frameworks have been developed to ensure responsible AI use in edu-
                   cational settings (Nguyen et al., 2022). These frameworks incorporate bias detection
                   algorithms and privacy protection measures to ensure ethical AI deployment.

                   Implications and practical applications
                   The comprehensive taxonomy of AI in educational leadership presented in this study has
                   significant implications for various stakeholders in the education sector. These impli-
                   cations extend beyond theoretical understanding, offering practical insights for educa-
                   tional leaders, policymakers, and researchers. For educational leaders, this taxonomy
                   serves as a roadmap for strategic AI implementation. By providing a structured overview
                   of AI applications across different domains of educational leadership, it enables leaders
                   to identify areas where AI can most effectively address their institution’s specific chal-
                   lenges and goals. For instance, a school district struggling with resource allocation might
                   focus on implementing AI solutions in the "Administrative Efficiency" and "Organiza-
                   tional Leadership and Strategic Planning" domains. Conversely, an institution aiming to
                   improve student outcomes might prioritize AI applications in the "Personalized Learn-
                   ing" and "Enhancing Teaching Practices" categories.

                   full spectrum of AI applications in education. Such programs could help leaders develop
                   a well-rounded understanding of AI’s potential and challenges, enabling them to make
                   informed decisions about AI adoption and implementation (Banerjee et al., 2021).

                   funding initiatives cover the full range of AI applications in education, from administra-
                   tive tools to learning analytics systems (Deo et al., 2020).

                   Reflections on the future of AI in educational leadership
                   As we consider the implications and applications of this taxonomy, it’s important to
                   reflect on the broader future of AI in educational leadership. The rapid pace of AI
                   development suggests that the landscape of education is likely to undergo significant
                   transformations in the coming years. One potential future direction is the emergence
                   of AI-augmented decision-making as a standard practice in educational leadership.
                   As AI systems become more sophisticated in analyzing complex educational data
                   and predicting outcomes, we may see a shift towards more data-driven leadership

                   approaches. However, this raises important questions about the balance between AI
                   driven insights and human judgment in educational decision-making (Fullan et al.,
                   2024).
                     Another area of potential growth is the development of more advanced personal-
                   ized learning systems. As AI technologies improve in their ability to understand and
                   adapt to individual learning styles and needs, we may see a shift towards more flex-
                   ible and personalized educational models. This could fundamentally change the role
                   of educational leaders, requiring them to orchestrate complex systems of human
                   and AI-driven instruction rather than managing traditional classroom-based models
                   (Seo et al., 2021). The increasing integration of AI in education also raises impor-
                   tant questions about the future of work in educational settings (Hojeij Kuhail & ElSa-
                   yary, 2024). As AI takes over more routine administrative tasks, the roles of teachers,
                   administrators, and support staff may evolve significantly. Educational leaders will
                   need to manage this transition, ensuring that AI complements rather than replaces
                   human expertise and that staff are supported in developing new skills and adapting to
                   changing job roles (Colombo et al., 2019).
                     Privacy and data ethics are likely to become even more critical concerns as AI sys-
                   tems in education become more pervasive. Educational leaders of the future may
                   need to become adept at managing complex data ecosystems, balancing the benefits
                   of data-driven insights with the imperative to protect student privacy and ensure eth-
                   ical use of data (Zhang et al., 2022).
                     The potential for AI to exacerbate or mitigate educational inequities will likely be a
                   central issue in the coming years (López Jiménez et al., 2023). Educational leaders will
                   need to be vigilant in ensuring that AI implementation does not widen existing gaps
                   and ideally works to narrow them. This may require a proactive approach to AI adop-
                   tion that prioritizes equity and inclusion from the outset (Meng & Sermsri, 2024).
                     As AI becomes more integrated into educational systems, there may also be a need
                   for new governance structures and leadership roles. We might see the emergence of
                   positions such as AI Ethics Officers or Educational Data Scientists as standard roles in
                   school district leadership teams (Wang, 2021).
                     Finally, the globalization of education, facilitated by AI-driven online learning plat-
                   forms, may require educational leaders to develop new skills in managing cross-cul-
                   tural and virtual learning environments. The ability to leverage AI to create inclusive
                   and effective global learning communities could become a key competency for educa-
                   tional leaders in the future (López Jiménez et al., 2021).
                     These reflections on the future underscore the importance of continued research
                   and dialogue on AI in educational leadership. As we move forward, it will be impor-
                   tant to regularly revisit and refine our understanding of how AI is shaping educational
                   leadership, always keeping in mind the fundamental goal of improving educational
                   outcomes and experiences for all learners. The taxonomy presented in this study pro-
                   vides a foundation for these ongoing discussions and investigations, offering a struc-
                   tured way to think about the multifaceted impacts of AI on educational leadership.
                   As we navigate the exciting and challenging future of AI in education, this framework
                   can serve as a valuable guide, helping us to harness the potential of AI while remain-
                   ing mindful of its complexities and ethical implications.
