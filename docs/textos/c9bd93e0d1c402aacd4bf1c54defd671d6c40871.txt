                                            Abstract: In the dynamic landscape of education, the role of school leadership is pivotal in shaping
                                            the experiences of both teachers and students. This research delves into the multifaceted relation-
                                            ship between the principal's leadership style, teacher performance, and learning quality. Through a
                                            combination of surveys, document analysis, and statistical analysis, this study explores the impact
                                            of various leadership styles on the educational environment. The findings underscore the profound
                                            influence of transformational leadership on teacher performance. Transformational leaders inspire
                                            and motivate teachers, fostering a collaborative and innovative atmosphere in which educators
                                            thrive. The positive correlation between transformational leadership and teacher performance con-
                                            tributes to improved learning quality, characterized by higher levels of student engagement, critical
                                            thinking, and academic achievement. Furthermore, the research recognizes the mediating role of
                                            school climate, emphasizing the significance of a positive and inclusive school environment in en-
                                            hancing teacher motivation and job satisfaction. It also highlights potential challenges associated
                                            with laissez-faire and transactional leadership styles, emphasizing the need for support and long-
                                            term teacher commitment. This research expands our understanding of educational leadership the-
                                            ory and paves the way for the development of new theories that encompass the complex interplay
                                            between leadership styles, teacher performance, and learning quality. It offers a roadmap for trans-
                                            forming educational practices, policies, and theories to create a more dynamic, inclusive, and effec-
                                            tive educational system. As the educational landscape continues to evolve, the findings of this re-
                                            search hold the potential to inspire change and innovation, ultimately shaping the future of educa-
                                            tion and enriching the lives of students and educators alike.

                                            Keywords: Educational Leadership; Principal's Leadership Style; Teacher Performance; Learning
                                            Quality; Transformational Leadership

                                      the future(Greer, 2012). Research has consistently shown that effective school leadership

                                      2.2 Conceptual Framework
                                          In this study, we propose a conceptual framework that explores the complex interplay
                                      between the principal's leadership style, teacher performance, and learning quality in ed-
                                      ucational settings. The framework consists of three central components:
                                          a. Independent Variable: Principal's Leadership Style
                                               The principal's leadership style is the primary independent variable under exam-
                                               ination. Leadership styles include transformational, transactional, laissez-faire,
                                               and democratic leadership, each characterized by distinct behaviors, attitudes,
                                               and approaches. The choice of leadership style by the principal is expected to sig-
                                               nificantly influence the school's organizational culture and climate.
                                          b. Mediating Variable: School Climate and Teacher Motivation
                                               We posit that the principal's leadership style indirectly influences teacher perfor-
                                               mance and, subsequently, learning quality through the mediating variables of
                                               school climate and teacher motivation.
                                               • School Climate: School climate encompasses the overall atmosphere, culture,
                                                    and interpersonal relationships within the school. A positive school climate
                                                    is expected to foster teacher job satisfaction and create an environment con-
                                                    ducive to effective teaching and learning.
                                               • Teacher Motivation: Teacher motivation is a critical factor in driving perfor-
                                                    mance and instructional quality. Principals who exhibit specific leadership
                                                    styles can influence teacher motivation by supporting autonomy, compe-
                                                    tence, and relatedness needs.
                                          c. Dependent Variables: Teacher Performance and Learning Quality
                                               • Teacher Performance: Teacher performance is a key outcome variable in this
                                                    framework. It refers to the quality of teaching, instructional methods, class-
                                                    room management, and teacher effectiveness. It is influenced by the princi-
                                                    pal's leadership style, mediated through school climate and teacher motiva-
                                                    tion.
                                               • Learning Quality: Learning quality pertains to the educational outcomes and
                                                    experiences of students. It encompasses academic achievement, student en-
                                                    gagement, and the development of critical thinking and problem-solving
                                                    skills. The quality of learning is influenced by both teacher performance and
                                                    the broader school environment shaped by the principal's leadership style.
                                      2.3 Principal's Leadership
                                           At the heart of a principal's leadership is their capacity to articulate a compelling vi-
                                      sion for the school. They set the direction, define the mission, and create a shared sense of
                                      purpose(Donaldson, 2006)(Portin et al., 2003). A visionary leader inspires the entire school
                                      community with a clear and inspiring image of what can be achieved. This vision serves
                                      as a guiding light, aligning efforts and resources toward a common goal(Smith &
                                      Andrews, 1989).
                                           A principal's vision goes beyond achieving academic excellence; it encompasses the
                                      creation of a school culture that values diversity, fosters inclusivity, and promotes holistic
                                      development. In this vision, educational excellence is not limited to high test scores but
                                      includes nurturing well-rounded individuals equipped with critical thinking, problem-
                                      solving, and social skills(Zollers et al., 1999).
                                           Effective school leadership empowers teachers, recognizing that they are the back-
                                      bone of the educational system(Broin, 2015). Principals who prioritize teacher develop-
                                      ment and well-being create an environment where educators feel valued and supported.
                                      This empowerment extends to providing opportunities for professional growth, fostering
                                      collaboration, and celebrating the achievements of teachers(Khalil et al., 2013).

Jurnal Ilmu Pendidikan dan Humaniora, 2022, Vol. 11, No. 3                                                                210 of 15




                                           Empowered teachers are more likely to be motivated, innovative, and committed to
                                      their profession(Bogler & Somech, 2004)(Darling-Hammond et al., 2017). They bring pas-
                                      sion and dedication to the classroom, which, in turn, enriches the educational experience
                                      for students. A principal's leadership sets the stage for creating a dynamic and engaged
                                      teaching force.
                                           The principal's leadership also plays a pivotal role in shaping the school's climate and
                                      culture(Leithwood & Jantzi, 1990)(Deal & Peterson, 2016). A positive school climate is one
                                      where students feel safe, respected, and motivated to learn. Principals who prioritize cre-
                                      ating such an environment recognize that the emotional and social well-being of students
                                      is inseparable from their academic success.
                                           By fostering a positive school climate, a principal contributes to an atmosphere where
                                      students are more likely to thrive academically and personally(Kutsyuruba et al., 2015).
                                      Such a climate encourages student engagement, reduces behavioral issues, and supports
                                      the development of crucial life skills.
                                      2.4 Teacher Performance
                                           Teacher performance refers to the effectiveness and quality of teaching delivered by
                                      educators(Stronge, 2018). It encompasses a wide range of factors, including instructional
                                      strategies, classroom management, assessment methods, and the ability to engage and
                                      inspire students. Effective teacher performance is not solely measured by standardized
                                      test scores but is reflected in the overall growth and development of students(Barr & Tagg,
                                      1995).
                                           a. The Impact on Student Outcomes:
                                               Teacher performance plays a fundamental role in shaping student outcomes.
                                               When teachers are effective in their roles, students benefit in various ways(Langer
                                               & Applebee, 1987): Academic Achievement: Effective teachers have a direct and
                                               positive impact on student academic achievement. They use innovative instruc-
                                               tional strategies, adapt to diverse learning needs, and ensure that students grasp
                                               core concepts and skills. Critical Thinking: Beyond rote memorization, effective
                                               teachers encourage critical thinking, problem-solving, and creativity. They foster
                                               a deep understanding of subjects and the ability to apply knowledge to real-world
                                               situations. Student Engagement: Engaging teaching methods and a positive class-
                                               room atmosphere make students more enthusiastic about learning. This engage-
                                               ment can lead to better attendance, participation, and retention of information.
                                               Emotional and Social Development: Teachers play a vital role in the emotional
                                               and social development of students. They model positive behaviors, promote em-
                                               pathy, and create a safe and inclusive classroom environment.
                                           b. Factors Influencing Teacher Performance:
                                               Teacher performance is influenced by a multitude of factors, and understanding
                                               these influences is essential for improving the quality of education(Brownell et
                                               al., 2011):
                                               Creating an optimal environment for teacher performance involves several key
                                               components. Continuous professional development is imperative, encompassing
                                               ongoing training, workshops, and access to resources to ensure educators remain
                                               abreast of the latest best practices and innovative teaching techniques. Supportive
                                               leadership from principals and school administrators is crucial, as it involves rec-
                                               ognizing and appreciating the efforts of teachers, fostering a collaborative atmos-
                                               phere, and providing the necessary resources for success. A positive school cul-
                                               ture, which values teamwork, diversity, and inclusivity, has a profound impact,
                                               as it fosters motivation, appreciation, and support among teachers. Building
                                               strong student-teacher relationships is essential, where trust, communication, and
                                               empathy facilitate improved student engagement and overall performance. Ade-
                                               quate resources, classroom materials, and technology are indispensable for effec-
                                               tive teaching, as a lack of necessary tools can impede teacher performance. Fi-
                                               nally, allowing teachers a degree of autonomy in their teaching methods, along
                                               with opportunities for collaboration with colleagues, often leads to heightened

                                                motivation and effectiveness. When these elements are thoughtfully integrated,
                                                they create an environment that nurtures and empowers educators to excel in
                                                their roles, fostering an atmosphere of continuous growth and learning.
                                      2.5 Learning Quality
                                          Learning quality refers to the effectiveness and depth of learning that students expe-
                                      rience(Stansfield et al., 2004). It encompasses various aspects, including the acquisition of
                                      knowledge, the development of critical thinking skills, problem-solving abilities, infor-
                                      mation retention, and the transfer of knowledge to real-world contexts(Anazifa & Djukri,
                                      2017). High learning quality is marked by students' capacity to not only memorize facts
                                      but to analyze, evaluate, and apply what they've learned.
                                          a. The Impact on Student Outcomes:
                                              The quality of learning is central to student success and future prospects. When
                                              students experience high learning quality, they benefit in numerous ways: Aca-
                                              demic Excellence: Learning quality is strongly correlated with academic achieve-
                                              ment. When students deeply comprehend subject matter and can apply it effec-
                                              tively, they perform better on standardized tests and exams. Critical Thinking:
                                              Quality learning nurtures critical thinking skills, enabling students to analyze in-
                                              formation, question assumptions, and approach problems with creative solutions.
                                              It encourages intellectual curiosity and adaptability. Problem Solving: Students
                                              with a strong foundation in learning quality are better equipped to tackle real-
                                              world challenges. They can apply their knowledge and skills to solve complex
                                              problems and make informed decisions. Lifelong Learning: Quality learning in-
                                              stills a love for learning that extends beyond the classroom. It motivates students
                                              to seek out new information, skills, and experiences throughout their lives, pro-
                                              moting lifelong learning.
                                          b. Factors Influencing Learning Quality:
                                              Learning quality is influenced by a range of factors, each playing a crucial role in
                                              shaping the educational experience:
                                              Teaching Methods: Effective teaching methods that foster active learning, engage-
                                              ment, and critical thinking contribute to learning quality. Educators who employ
                                              innovative strategies, encourage discussion, and tailor instruction to students'
                                              needs enhance learning. Curriculum Design: A well-designed curriculum that
                                              emphasizes depth over breadth can promote learning quality. It allows for the
                                              exploration of concepts in greater detail, encouraging a comprehensive under-
                                              standing. Assessment and Feedback: Continuous and constructive assessment, as
                                              well as timely feedback, provide students with insights into their progress. It
                                              helps them recognize areas for improvement and reinforces effective learning
                                              strategies. Educational Resources: Access to appropriate educational resources,
                                              including textbooks, technology, and supplementary materials, is essential for
                                              quality learning. A lack of resources can hinder the depth and breadth of learning.
                                              School Climate and Support: A positive school climate that promotes inclusivity,
                                              values diversity, and provides emotional support fosters high learning quality.
                                              Students who feel safe, respected, and encouraged are more likely to engage in
                                              meaningful learning experiences. Student Engagement: Engaged students are
                                              more likely to experience high learning quality. Encouraging active participation,
                                              curiosity, and exploration stimulates the depth of understanding.
                                      2.6 Leadership Styles
                                           In the realm of education, leadership is not a one-size-fits-all concept but a multifac-
                                      eted and dynamic force that shapes the culture and climate of schools(Mulford, 2008).
                                      Educational leadership styles are pivotal in determining the direction and effectiveness of
                                      a school community(Slater & Teddlie, 1992). Principals, as the leaders of these communi-
                                      ties, embody various leadership styles, each with distinct approaches and philoso-
                                      phies(Bush & Glover, 2003).

Jurnal Ilmu Pendidikan dan Humaniora, 2022, Vol. 11, No. 3                                                                212 of 15




                                           Transformational Leadership: Transformational leadership is characterized by its in-
                                      spirational and visionary approach(Deluga, 1990). These leaders inspire and motivate oth-
                                      ers through a compelling vision of the future, fostering a sense of purpose and commit-
                                      ment. In education, transformational leaders promote innovation, encourage creativity,
                                      and create a collaborative learning environment. They emphasize personal growth, au-
                                      tonomy, and intrinsic motivation, which can lead to higher teacher morale and student
                                      achievement(Khanin, 2007).
                                           Transactional Leadership: Transactional leadership, in contrast, operates on a system
                                      of rewards and punishments. Leaders set clear expectations, establish performance stand-
                                      ards, and provide recognition and rewards for compliance. This style can be effective in
                                      the short term, ensuring that tasks are completed and discipline is maintained. However,
                                      it may lack the capacity to inspire long-term commitment or intrinsic motivation in teach-
                                      ers and students(Doherty & Danylchuk, 1996).
                                           Laissez-Faire Leadership: Laissez-faire leadership takes a hands-off approach, giving
                                      autonomy and responsibility to subordinates. In the educational context, this style can
                                      empower teachers to take charge of their classrooms and instructional decisions. How-
                                      ever, it can also lead to a lack of guidance and direction, potentially resulting in incon-
                                      sistency and disarray(Eskola, 2011).
                                           Democratic Leadership: Democratic leaders involve teachers, staff, and sometimes
                                      students in the decision-making process. This style promotes shared governance, open
                                      communication, and collaborative problem-solving. It can lead to a sense of ownership
                                      and engagement within the school community, with a focus on inclusivity and consensus-
                                      building.
                                           Situational Leadership: Situational leadership adapts to the specific needs of a situa-
                                      tion or individual. Leaders may switch between different styles depending on the circum-
                                      stances. This flexibility can be effective in addressing diverse challenges within a school,
                                      ensuring that the leadership style aligns with the specific needs of teachers and students.
                                           Servant Leadership: Servant leadership places the leader in a role of service to others.
                                      This style prioritizes the well-being and growth of teachers and students, emphasizing
                                      empathy, humility, and ethical behavior. Servant leaders create a supportive and caring
                                      environment, fostering trust and collaboration.
                                           Inclusive Leadership: Inclusive leadership values diversity and inclusion. Leaders ac-
                                      tively work to create an environment that welcomes individuals of all backgrounds, abil-
                                      ities, and perspectives. This style aims to eliminate barriers, promote equity, and ensure
                                      that all students and staff have an equal opportunity to succeed.

                                      3.   Results and Discussion
                                      3.1 Result
                                          In the dynamic landscape of education, the role of leadership styles within school en-
                                      vironments has a profound influence on teacher performance and learning quality. The
                                      research conducted in this study sought to explore the relationships between leadership
                                      styles exhibited by school principals, teacher performance, and learning quality. The main
                                      findings from this research include:
                                          a. Transformational Leadership and Educational Excellence:
                                               • The research reveals a significant positive correlation between transforma-
                                                   tional leadership and teacher performance. Principals who exhibit transfor-
                                                   mational leadership behaviors, including inspiring and motivating teachers
                                                   through a compelling vision, create an environment that encourages teacher
                                                   innovation, collaboration, and commitment.
                                               • Students in schools led by transformational principals consistently demon-
                                                   strated higher levels of engagement, critical thinking skills, and academic
                                                   achievement. The positive impact of this leadership style on learning quality
                                                   is evident in well-rounded, inquisitive, and high-achieving students.
                                          b. Transactional Leadership and Short-Term Compliance:
                                               • Transactional leadership showed effectiveness in ensuring short-term teacher
                                                   compliance and task completion. This style, characterized by clear expecta-
                                                   tions and rewards, may be useful in managing day-to-day school operations.
                                               • However, it did not foster the same level of teacher motivation, creativity, and
                                                   commitment as transformational leadership. The research suggests that trans-
                                                   actional leadership alone may not be sufficient for long-term educational ex-
                                                   cellence.
                                          c. Laissez-Faire Leadership and Potential Challenges:
                                               • Laissez-faire leadership, a hands-off approach, led to mixed results. While it
                                                   could empower teachers and promote autonomy, it also presented chal-
                                                   lenges, including a lack of direction and inconsistency in teaching practices.
                                               • In schools led by laissez-faire principals, teacher performance varied widely,
                                                   impacting learning quality. The research suggests that this leadership style
                                                   requires additional structures and support to ensure consistency and effec-
                                                   tiveness.
                                          d. The Influence of School Climate and Teacher Motivation:
                                               • School climate, shaped by leadership style, emerged as a significant mediat-
                                                   ing factor. Positive school climate, characterized by collaboration, inclusivity,
