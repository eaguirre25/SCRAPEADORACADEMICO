ABSTRACT
This study examines the digital transformation in the management of Islamic Boarding Schools
(Pondok Pesantren/Ponpes) and its impact on the quality of educational services in three Islamic
Boarding Schools in Lampung. The research uses a descriptive qualitative approach with a
multi-site case study at Ponpes Terpadu Ushuluddin (South Lampung), Ponpes Al-Hikmah (Bandar
Lampung), and Ponpes Nurul Huda (Pringsewu). Data were collected through observation, in-depth
interviews with 20 key informants, and documentary studies during the April-October 2024 period.
The results show significant variations in digitalization implementation: Ponpes Terpadu
Ushuluddin adopted a holistic approach with technology integration in administration, Arabic
language learning, and communication; Ponpes Al-Hikmah implemented selective digitalization as
a complement to traditional methods; while Ponpes Nurul Huda remains in the early stages with
digitalization limited to basic communication. Digitalization has proven to increase administrative
efficiency by 40%, expand learning access through digital platforms, and enrich Arabic teaching
methods through interactive multimedia. Positive impacts are seen in increased student learning
motivation (85%), administrative time efficiency (35%), and management transparency. However,
this transformation faces serious challenges including digital infrastructure gaps (especially in rural
areas), limitations in managers' technological competence (60% of managers are untrained), cultural
dilemmas between modernity and tradition, and budget constraints. This study recommends
developing specific digital roadmaps for each pesantren typology, tiered training programs for
managers, synergy between local government policies and national programs, and strengthening
sustainable digital infrastructure. The research implications provide theoretical contributions to
digital transformation models in value-based educational institutions and practical contributions for
pesantren managers in designing contextual digital strategies.

Keywords: Digital Transformation, Islamic Boarding Schools, Educational Services, Arabic
Language Learning, Educational Management.


1.​ INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background
         Islamic Boarding Schools (Pondok Pesantren/Ponpes) as the oldest Islamic educational
institutions in Indonesia have undergone a long and dynamic historical journey. From the
pre-independence period to the reform era, pesantren have functioned not only as centers for
religious knowledge transmission (tafaqquh fi al-din) but also as agents of social change, guardians
of Islamic scholarly traditions, and laboratories for character development (Azra, 2001; Dhofier,
1997). The uniqueness of pesantren lies in their ability to survive various historical changes while
maintaining their distinctive identity based on spiritual values, learning communities, and the
integration of religious knowledge with daily life (Mastuhu, 1994; Bruinessen, 1995).

        In Lampung Province, the existence of pesantren plays a strategic role in the educational
ecosystem. According to data from the Ministry of Religious Affairs of Lampung Province (2023),
there are over 600 Islamic Boarding Schools spread across 15 districts/cities with various
typologies, ranging from salaf (traditional) pesantren that maintain the classical book (kitab kuning)
learning system, modern pesantren that integrate general curriculum, to integrated pesantren that
combine both. This diversity reflects the adaptive capacity of pesantren in responding to local
socio-cultural needs and global challenges.
        The 21st century brings an inevitable wave of digital transformation in all aspects of life,
including education. The Industrial Revolution 4.0 and Society 5.0 demand educational institutions
to adapt to digital technology not as an option but as a strategic necessity to maintain relevance and
competitiveness (Schwab, 2016; Xu et al., 2018). In this context, pesantren face complex dilemmas:
on one hand, they must respond to modernity and digitalization demands; on the other hand, they
must preserve their distinctive scholarly traditions that have proven effective for centuries (Hasan,
2015; Mas'ud, 2006).
        Digitalization of pesantren education covers a broad spectrum, ranging from administrative
and management transformation, learning innovation, digital content development, to the formation
of an integrated digital ecosystem (Fatah et al., 2015; Bush, 2008). The pesantren digitalization
programs launched by Islamic community organizations such as Nahdlatul Ulama (NU) through
PBNU and Muhammadiyah through the "Digital Pesantren" program emphasize the importance of
technology adaptation in maintaining pesantren continuity and development in the digital era
(PBNU, 2022; Muhammadiyah, 2021).
        A special focus in this research is the impact of digitalization on Arabic language learning as
the core curriculum of pesantren. Arabic functions not only as a communication tool but more
importantly as a key to accessing classical Islamic scholarly treasures (turats) that form the
foundation of pesantren education (Izzan, 2011; Baharuddin, 2014). However, various studies show
that Arabic language learning in many pesantren still faces methodological challenges, including:
(1) dominance of grammar-translation methods that lack encouragement for active communication;
(2) limitations of attractive learning media and resources; (3) minimal exposure to language use in
authentic communicative contexts; and (4) gaps between mastery of nahwu-sharaf theory and
practical communication skills (Hidayat, 2012; Makinudin, 2015).
        This is where digital technology offers transformative potential. The Multimedia Learning
Theory (Mayer, 2020) explains that the combination of text, images, audio, and video can enhance
understanding and memory retention. The Communicative Language Teaching approach supported
by digital technology can create contextual, interactive, and personalized learning environments
(Richards & Rodgers, 2014; Warschauer, 2017). Digital platforms such as language learning apps,
video conferences, virtual labs, and multimedia content can overcome spatial and temporal
limitations while providing rich and diverse learning resources.
        However, implementing digitalization in pesantren is not without challenges. Previous
research identifies several structural and cultural barriers, including: (1) digital infrastructure gaps
between urban and rural pesantren; (2) budget limitations for technology investment; (3) resistance
from traditionalists who fear the negative impacts of technology on pesantren values; (4) lack of
digital competence among managers and teachers; and (5) challenges in maintaining balance
between digital innovation and tradition preservation (Nasir, 2005; Nur Efendi, 2014; Musthofa,
2015).

2.5 Data Analysis : Interactive Model of Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña (2018)
         Data analysis followed the interactive, cyclical model proposed by Miles, Huberman, and
Saldaña (2018), which comprises three concurrent and interrelated components rather than strictly
linear phases. First, Data Reduction involved systematically condensing the voluminous raw
data—comprising interview transcripts, detailed observation notes, and various documents—into
manageable and meaningful units. This was achieved through a multi-step process: initial Coding,
where labels were assigned to significant segments of text; subsequent Categorization, where
related codes were grouped into broader thematic categories; and finally Abstraction, where these
categories were synthesized into overarching, general themes that captured the core essence of the
data. The qualitative data analysis software NVivo 12 was utilized to support and enhance the
efficiency and rigor of this coding and organizational process.
         Second, Data Display focused on organizing and presenting the condensed information in
formats that facilitated the identification of patterns and relationships. This was accomplished
through several visualization tools: Descriptive Narratives provided rich, contextual accounts for
each individual case study; Comparative Matrices enabled side-by-side analysis of the three sites
across key thematic dimensions; Thematic Networks graphically illustrated the connections and
hierarchies between emergent themes; and Flowcharts mapped the procedural logic and stages of
the digital transformation process within each pesantren.
         Third, Conclusion Drawing and Verification constituted the interpretative phase.
Conclusions were generated through analytical techniques such as Pattern Matching, which
involved aligning observed empirical patterns with existing theoretical frameworks; Explanation
Building, an iterative process of developing and refining causal narratives to explain the studied
phenomena; and Cross-Case Analysis, which synthesized findings across sites to distinguish
between universal patterns and context-specific variations. To ensure the trustworthiness of these
conclusions, rigorous verification strategies were employed, including Triangulation of data sources
and methods, Member Checking with informants to validate interpretations, maintaining a
comprehensive Audit Trail for methodological transparency, and engaging in Peer Debriefing to
critically review findings with colleagues.

2.5.1 Research Ethics
        Adherence to stringent ethical principles was paramount throughout the study. Informed
Consent was secured from all participants via written agreements that clearly outlined the research
scope and their rights. Confidentiality was rigorously maintained by anonymizing all participants
and institutions with pseudonyms. The research was guided by the Beneficence Principle, aiming to
yield constructive insights for the participating communities, while the Non-maleficence Principle
ensured that no harm or undue burden was placed on participants. The Justice Principle governed
the research conduct, guaranteeing equitable and respectful treatment for everyone involved.

2.5.2 Validity and Reliability
        The study implemented established qualitative research strategies to bolster its validity and

3.​ RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Profiles and Variations of Digital Transformation in Three Ponpes
       Comparative analysis shows a broad spectrum of digital transformation across the three
ponpes, with variations in depth, speed, and implementation focus.

3.1.1 Ponpes Terpadu Ushuluddin: Holistic Integration Model
         Ponpes Terpadu Ushuluddin represents the most advanced digital transformation model
among the three sites. Digitalization implementation was conducted holistically covering four main
pillars:

     a.​ Administration and Management Digitalization:
         In the aspect of administration and management, the pesantren has implemented a simple,
self-developed Pesantren Management Information System (SIMP). This system includes several
core modules: an Integrated Student Database for recording personal, academic, health, and
financial student data; a Digital Financial System enabling real-time recording of income (such as
boarding fees and donations) and expenses; a Digital Inventory for asset tracking via QR codes; and
a Digital Attendance system for students and teachers utilizing fingerprint technology.
Implementation results show a 35% increase in administrative time efficiency based on comparisons
before and after the system's adoption. As stated by Kiai Udin, "Financial reports previously took 3
days to complete; now they take only 2 hours. Missing or inaccurate student data no longer exists."

     b.​ Arabic Language Learning Transformation:
         The transformation of Arabic language learning was executed through a multimodal
approach. A Digital Language Laboratory has been established, equipped with 25 computers
installed with Arabic learning software such as Rosetta Stone Arabic and Arabic Online.
Additionally, a Hybrid Class model has been implemented, combining face-to-face learning with
online materials via the Google Classroom platform. The development of Multimedia Content has
also been a focus, including the creation of animated nahwu-sharaf learning videos, Arabic
conversation podcasts, and Arabic-language educational games. Learning assessment has been
converted to Digital Assessment using platforms like Quizizz and Google Forms, which feature
automatic correction. The impact is evident from surveys indicating increased student learning
motivation, with 85% of students reporting greater motivation to learn Arabic through digital media.
Achievements in regional Arabic language competitions have also increased by 40% over the past
two years.

     c.​ Digital Infrastructure:
         The pesantren allocated 15% of its annual budget for the development of digital
infrastructure. The established Internet Network utilizes fiber optics with a speed of 100 Mbps and
WiFi coverage reaching 90% of the boarding school area. The Computer-to-student device ratio is
1:10, with each classroom equipped with a projector, and tablets provided for senior teachers.

3.1.2 Ponpes Al-Hikmah: Selective Modernization Model
       Ponpes Al-Hikmah adopts a selective and gradual digital transformation approach, with the
philosophy of "modernization that does not erode tradition." Digitalization implementation focuses
on the most strategic areas:

    a.​ Digitalization as Complement to Traditional Learning:
        Digitalization in this context is implemented not as a replacement for, but as a
complementary enhancement to, established traditional pedagogies. This approach is exemplified
through several specific initiatives: the digitization of 200 classical books (kitab kuning) into PDF
and EPUB formats to facilitate easier access; the creation and distribution of Audio Studies,
comprising recordings of classical book discourses by senior kiai, disseminated through WhatsApp
groups; and the provision of Limited Online References granting access to Islamic scholarly
databases such as al-maktaba.org and shamelah.org. Crucially, the core instructional
methodologies—particularly the sorogan (individual tutorial) and bandongan (collective lecture)
methods used for deepening recitation skills (qira'ah) and grammatical mastery
(nahwu-sharaf)—remain firmly rooted in tradition, as they are regarded as essential for ensuring
profound knowledge acquisition (tafaqquh). Here, digitalization functions instrumentally as a tool to
reinforce, rather than fundamentally alter, the traditional educational framework. This cautious
integration is underpinned by a leadership style that is both instructional and charismatic, with
strong authority vested in the kiai. Consequently, any technological adoption undergoes meticulous
consideration to ensure it does not disrupt the focused learning environment and spiritual
concentration of the students.

    b.​ Limited Administration Digitalization:
        The digitalization of administrative processes remains relatively basic and circumscribed. It
encompasses the maintenance of a Simple Student Database using Excel spreadsheets for core
student information; the utilization of Digital Communication platforms, primarily WhatsApp
groups, for internal coordination among administrators and external communication with students'
guardians; and the employment of Digital Marketing via social media channels like Instagram and
Facebook for publicizing institutional activities and recruiting new students.

    c.​ Infrastructure and Constraints:
        The supporting digital infrastructure is notably constrained. The Internet Network is limited,
with WiFi access typically restricted to administrative offices and the library. The available Devices
are few, consisting of approximately five computers designated for administrative tasks and a single
projector for presentations. These limitations stem from significant Main Constraints, including a
severely limited budgetary allocation for technology—constituting only about 3% of total

    d.​ Inhibiting Factors:
        Several interrelated factors act as impediments to more extensive digital transformation.

a cautious approach to technological adoption. In summary, the transformation model at Ponpes
Al-Hikmah can be classified at the augmentation level within the SAMR framework; technology is
used to improve the efficiency of existing tasks but does not fundamentally redefine the core
essence of the learning process.

3.1.3 Ponpes Nurul Huda: Minimalist Adoption Model
        Ponpes Nurul Huda is at the earliest stage of digital transformation, which can be called
"practical digitalization" or "emergency digitalization" during the pandemic.

    a.​ Digitalization Limited to Communication:
        The extent of technological integration is confined primarily to communicative functions.
The implementation leverages WhatsApp as the operational backbone for virtually all internal
coordination among staff and external communication with students' families. For more formal or
special engagements, such as maintaining connections with alumni and donors (silaturahmi), the
platform Zoom is utilized occasionallyBasic public outreach and information dissemination are
managed through a simple Facebook page which serves to post activity updates.

    b.​ Learning Still Fully Traditional:
        There is a deliberate absence of technology integration within the core pedagogical
processes, especially for Arabic language instruction. Teaching relies entirely on Conventional
Methods with a pronounced emphasis on bandongan (collective lectures), sorogan (individual
student recitation to a teacher), and rote memorization. The Traditional Media in use are
fundamental: blackboards, printed books, and paper. Furthermore, Assessment is conducted
manually involving handwritten examinations that are subsequently corrected by hand, without any
digital aids.

    c.​ Structural Barriers:
        The obstacles to digital transformation are predominantly structural and infrastructural in
nature. The infrastructure is a significant challenge, given the pesantren's rural setting, which is
plagued by an unstable internet connection and frequent power outages. Financially, the Budget is
overwhelmingly allocated (approximately 95%) to covering basic operational costs such as food,
electricity, and teacher stipends, leaving virtually no dedicated funding for technological
investment. From a human capital perspective, there is a critical shortage of Human Resources with
technical expertise; the institution lacks staff proficient in technology, and the average educational
attainment among teachers is at the high school level or its equivalent.

    d.​ Perceptions of Digitalization:
        Attitudes towards digitalization among the leadership and community are ambivalent and
cautious. There exists a pronounced Skeptical Attitude epitomized by Kiai Huda's statement that
"Students come here for riyadhah (spiritual discipline and training), not to play gadgets." This
reflects a deep-seated concern that technology might contradict the institution's core spiritual

3.2 Implications of Digitalization on Arabic Language Learning
        Digitalization provides different impacts on the development of four Arabic language skills
(maharah) across the three ponpes, with significant variations according to technology adoption
levels.

3.2.1 Maharah Istima' (Listening Skill)
         Ponpes Ushuluddin has transformed listening learning through:
     1)​ Varied Audio Content: Collection of 500+ learning audio with accent variations (Egyptian,
         Saudi, Lebanese, Sudanese).
     2)​ Interactive Listening Applications: Software providing listening exercises with graded
         difficulty levels.
     3)​ Learning Podcasts: "Sama' al-Arabiyyah" - internally produced podcast with daily topics.
     4)​ Listening Laboratory: Special room with high-quality audio devices.

        Results showed 45% increase in student listening ability based on standardized tests.
Students can listen to Arabic speeches at normal speed and understand 70% content after 1 year of
training.

        Ponpes Al-Hikmah utilizes technology to enrich but not change core methods:
    1)​ Classical Book Study Audio: Recordings of classical book studies by kiai for repetition
        outside class.
    2)​ Digital Sermons: Collection of Friday sermons from various mosques in Arab world.
    3)​ Limited to Authentic Content: Focus on religious content rather than general learning
        materials.
        Ponpes Nurul Huda still relies entirely on:
    1)​ Direct Listening: Students listen directly to teacher readings and explanations.
    2)​ Traditional Media: Obsolete cassettes and CDs.

        Ponpes Al-Hikmah - Reading for Tradition Strengthening:
    1)​ Digital Classical Books: Priority on classical book digitalization.
    2)​ Digital Tafsir: Access to classical and contemporary tafsir.

3.3 Management Digitalization and Improvement of Non-Academic Services
      Management digitalization significantly impacts the quality of non-academic services in
ponpes that have adopted it.

3.3.1 Administrative Efficiency
         Ponpes Ushuluddin shows the most dramatic efficiency improvement:
     1)​ Student Data Processing: Student data search time from 15 minutes to 30 seconds.
     2)​ Salary Processing: Processing salaries for 25 employees from 2 days to 2 hours.
     3)​ Financial Reporting: Monthly report preparation from 5 days to 1 day.
     4)​ Inventory: Tracking 500+ asset items from manual to real-time digital.
         The system has saved an average of 120 working hours/month or equivalent to 3 FTE
(Full-Time Equivalent).

        Ponpes Al-Hikmah experiences moderate improvement:
    1)​ Communication with Student Guardians: 50% efficiency in information delivery.
    2)​ Online Registration: Reduces physical queues during new student admission.
    3)​ Still Semi-digital: Many processes still hybrid (digital and manual).

       Ponpes Nurul Huda has not felt significant benefits due to minimal digitalization.

3.3.2 Transparency and Accountability
         Digitalization significantly increases transparency:
     1)​ Open Financial Reports: Ponpes Ushuluddin publishes concise financial reports via website.
     2)​ Transparent Donation System: Donors can track donation usage via special portal.
     3)​ Online Complaint System: Student and guardian complaint system via application.
     4)​ Increased Trust: Student guardian satisfaction survey increased from 65% to 85% in 2 years.

3.3.3 Communication and Stakeholder Engagement
         Communication transformation is the most universal impact:
     1)​ WhatsApp as Universal Platform: Used by all three ponpes with different intensity.
     2)​ Social Media for Branding: Ponpes Ushuluddin and Al-Hikmah active on Instagram and
         Facebook.
     3)​ Website as Digital Face: Only Ushuluddin has functional website.
     4)​ Digital Newsletter: Regular updates for alumni and donors.

3.3.4 Digital Crisis Management
         The COVID-19 pandemic became a catalyst for digitalization in all ponpes:
     1)​ Ponpes Ushuluddin: Already prepared, switched to hybrid learning in 1 week.
     2)​ Ponpes Al-Hikmah: Forced to adopt Zoom and WhatsApp for learning continuity.
     3)​ Ponpes Nurul Huda: Almost closed, only survived with external assistance.
         The pandemic experience shows the importance of digital resilience - survival ability with
technology during crises.

3.5 Challenges, Dilemmas, and Innovative Solutions
        Digital transformation in pesantren is not free from various challenges and dilemmas
requiring creative solutions.

3.5.1 Dilemma Between Tradition and Modernity
       The most fundamental dilemma is how to modernize without losing traditional identity.
       Example Dilemmas:
       -​ Classical Books vs E-Books: Do e-book classical books reduce blessings?
       -​ Sorogan vs Video Conference: Does virtual learning reduce teacher-student connection
          (sanad)?
       -​ Handwriting vs Digital Typing: Does typing reduce contemplation in writing?

        Innovative Solutions at Ponpes Ushuluddin:
    1)​ Digital with Blessings: Every technology use begins with prayer and intention.
    2)​ Hybrid Model: Maintain physical sorogan, add virtual consultation.
    3)​ Digital Books with Takhrij: E-books complemented with digital sanad.
    4)​ Digital Calligraphy: Combining calligraphy art with digital technology.

3.5.2 Digital Divide
         The gap between urban and rural ponpes is widening.
         Divide Data:
     1)​ Internet Access: Ushuluddin (100 Mbps) vs Nurul Huda (2 Mbps, unstable).
     2)​ Devices: Ushuluddin (1:10) vs Nurul Huda (1:100).
     3)​ Competence: Ushuluddin (80% trained teachers) vs Nurul Huda (0% trained teachers).

        Implemented Solutions:
    1)​ Offline Solutions: Development of content accessible without internet.
    2)​ Mobile-First Strategy: Focus on more affordable smartphones.
    3)​ Resource Sharing: Urban ponpes help rural ponpes (Ushuluddin mentors Nurul Huda).
    4)​ Policy Advocacy: Urging government to pay attention to pesantren digital divide.

3.5.3 Digital Security and Ethics
         Pesantren face new challenges in digital security:
     1)​ Student Data Privacy: Risk of personal data leakage.
     2)​ Negative Content: Exposure to inappropriate content while browsing.
     3)​ Cyberbullying: Social media bullying among students.
     4)​ Plagiarism: Copy-paste in digital assignments.

        Developed Solutions:
    1)​ Digital Ethics Curriculum: Mandatory digital ethics curriculum for students.
    2)​ Filtering System: Content filtering system in ponpes network.
    3)​ Data Protection Policy: Strict data protection policy.
    4)​ Digital Detox Program: Periodic gadget-free for balance.

3.5.4 Sustainability
         Many digital initiatives fail due to sustainability issues.
         Common Failure Patterns:
     1)​ Pilot Projects: Digitalization as short-term project.

3.6 Digital Transformation Models for Pesantren
       Based on research findings, three digital transformation models for pesantren can be
formulated according to typology and readiness.

3.6.1 Holistic Integration Model (For Integrated/Modern Pesantren)
         Recipient Characteristics:
     1)​ Pesantren with clear modernization vision
     2)​ Visionary and technologically literate leadership
     3)​ Adequate resources (funds, infrastructure)
     4)​ Relatively young and open-to-change human resources

        Implementation Stages:
    1)​ Preparation Phase (6-12 months):
        -​ Digital vision and roadmap preparation
        -​ Readiness and needs assessment
        -​ Digital transformation team formation
        -​ Basic infrastructure preparation
    2)​ Core Implementation Phase (12-24 months):
        -​ Administration and management digitalization
        -​ Learning and curriculum transformation
        -​ Digital HR competency development
        -​ Integrated digital ecosystem building
    3)​ Optimization Phase (24+ months):
        -​ System refinement and scaling
        -​ New digital innovation development
        -​ Digital networking and collaboration expansion
        -​ Continuous evaluation and adjustment
        Key Components:
        -​ Leadership: Digital champion at leadership level
        -​ Governance: Clear digital governance structure
        -​ Infrastructure: Cloud-first, mobile-friendly infrastructure
        -​ Curriculum: Digital literacy as core competency
        -​ Culture: Growth mindset, experimentation culture

3.6.2 Selective Modernization Model (For Modern-Salaf Pesantren)
       Recipient Characteristics:
       -​ Pesantren wanting to preserve tradition but need modernization
       -​ Moderate resource limitations
       -​ Mixed human resources (old-young)
       -​ Moderate change resistance

4. CONCLUSION
        Based on in-depth analysis of three Islamic boarding schools in Lampung with different
typologies, this research concludes several key points:
        First, digital transformation of ponpes management in Lampung occurs in a highly varied
spectrum, forming a continuum from minimalist digitalization to holistic integration. This variation
is primarily determined by three dominant factors: (1) vision and leadership of kiai/pesantren
leaders; (2) resource availability (infrastructure, budget, human resources); and (3) educational
philosophy adopted (tradition preservation vs modern adaptation). Ponpes Terpadu Ushuluddin
represents a holistic integration model with comprehensive transformation in four pillars
(administration, learning, infrastructure, human resources). Ponpes Al-Hikmah adopts a selective
modernization model with technology as tradition complement. While Ponpes Nurul Huda is at the
practical digitalization stage focusing on basic communication.
        Second, digitalization provides significant positive impacts on educational service quality,
but with variations according to adoption levels. In the context of Arabic language learning,
digitalization plays roles in: (a) enriching and varying input for listening skills through multimedia;
(b) opening creative spaces for speaking practice through vlogs and podcasts; (c) expanding access
to digital texts for reading skill strengthening; and (d) increasing efficiency and productivity in
writing skills. Ponpes with holistic integration (Ushuluddin) show 45-300% improvements in
various skill aspects, while ponpes with limited adoption show moderate or insignificant
improvements.
        Third, beyond learning, digitalization dramatically improves operational efficiency. Digital
administration reduces process time by up to 35%, increases data accuracy, and strengthens
transparency. Digital communication accelerates and expands interaction with stakeholders. Digital
branding improves institutional image and appeal. However, these benefits are only fully felt by
pesantren that have reached certain adoption levels.

        Fourth, digital transformation faces complex multidimensional barriers. Structural barriers
(infrastructure limitations, budget, regulations) are most severe in rural and traditional pesantren.
Cultural barriers (change resistance, tradition-modernity dilemmas, concerns about negative
impacts) appear in all typologies but with different intensities. Competence barriers (generation
gaps, lack of training, fixed mindset) become main obstacles to optimal technology utilization.
        Fifth, successful digital transformation requires contextual, participatory, and value-oriented
approaches. There is no one-size-fits-all model for all pesantren. Approaches must be adapted to
each pesantren's typology, readiness, resources, and philosophy. Success keys include: visionary
leadership directly involved, clear and realistic roadmap, gradual competency improvement,
continuous dialogue about values and traditions, and sustainable support systems.
        Sixth, pesantren digital transformation is not an ultimate goal, but a means to strengthen
pesantren's educational mission: producing students with deep religious knowledge (tafaqquh fi
al-din), noble character, and ability to live relevantly in the digital era. Technology must be
tradition's servant, not replacement. Successful digitalization strengthens, not weakens, pesantren's
basic identity and values.
