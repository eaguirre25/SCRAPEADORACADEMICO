                                           Abstract
This study aims to develop a conceptual framework of adaptive Islamic educational
leadership for school principals in responding to digital transformation. The study
employs a qualitative library research method through systematic conceptual analysis and
synthesis of literature on digital leadership, adaptive leadership, educational management,
Islamic educational leadership, organizational transformation, teacher digital
competence, and technology integration. The findings indicate that digital transformation
requires school principals to move beyond conventional administrative functions toward
strategic, pedagogical, technological, organizational, and ethical leadership. The
proposed framework comprises eight interconnected competencies: digital vision,
adaptive intelligence, pedagogical-digital leadership, collaborative empowerment, data-
informed decision-making, innovation and change management, ethical-digital
governance, and continuous professional learning. These competencies are integrated
with Islamic moral-spiritual principles to ensure that technological transformation
remains aligned with educational objectives, human development, ethical responsibility,
and Islamic values. The novelty of this study lies in the formulation of an adaptive Islamic
educational leadership framework that integrates digital and adaptive leadership
perspectives with Islamic educational principles, positioning school principals as strategic
agents of pedagogical, organizational, cultural, and ethical transformation. The
framework demonstrates that digital transformation in Islamic educational institutions
should be understood not merely as technology adoption but as a comprehensive
transformation of leadership and educational practice.
Keywords: Islamic Educational Leadership, Digital Transformation, School
Management, Adaptive Leadership, Educational Innovation.

Introduction
        Digital transformation has fundamentally changed the organizational landscape of
education. Schools are increasingly expected to integrate digital platforms, artificial
intelligence, learning management systems, data analytics, cloud-based administration,
and online communication into their daily operations. This transformation has altered not
only teaching practices but also the responsibilities of educational leaders. School
principals must now make decisions concerning technology infrastructure, digital
learning, teacher competence, cybersecurity, data governance, and organizational
innovation. Håkansson Lindqvist and Pettersson (2019) demonstrate that digitalization

        The expansion of digital technology has consequently changed the meaning of
educational leadership. In a traditional school organization, principals may have
concentrated on administrative coordination, personnel management, curriculum
implementation, and supervision. In a digitally transforming institution, these
responsibilities remain important but are accompanied by demands for technological
vision, digital communication, data interpretation, innovation management, and
organizational agility. Recent systematic reviews show that digital leadership is
associated with school effectiveness and organizational transformation. Okunlola (2025),
for example, reported a positive relationship between digital leadership and school
effectiveness across empirical studies. This evidence indicates that principals are not
peripheral actors in digital transformation but central organizational agents who
determine how technology becomes educationally meaningful.
        The challenge becomes more distinctive within Islamic educational institutions.
Islamic schools and madrasahs are expected to respond to technological change while
maintaining religious identity, moral integrity, and educational traditions. Digitalization
can create opportunities for improving access to Islamic learning, strengthening
communication, developing innovative pedagogy, and expanding institutional networks.
At the same time, it can generate challenges involving misinformation, inappropriate
content, weakened interpersonal relationships, excessive screen use, commercialization,
privacy, and the erosion of ethical boundaries. Recent studies of Islamic educational
leadership argue that digital transformation requires adaptive, visionary, innovative, and
value-oriented leadership rather than technology-oriented management alone (Rizki &
Junaidi, 2026; Wibowo et al., 2025). These findings suggest that Islamic educational
leadership requires a synthesis between technological competence and normative
responsibility.
        The principal therefore occupies a strategic position in determining whether
digital transformation becomes meaningful educational innovation or merely
technological modernization. A school may possess sophisticated hardware and software
while producing limited educational improvement if teachers lack competence,
organizational culture resists change, or digital tools are disconnected from pedagogical
objectives. Håkansson Lindqvist (2019) found that school leaders can create beneficial
conditions for technology-enhanced learning, but they also encounter technical problems,
teacher-support demands, and limitations of leadership time. The implication is that
technology implementation requires leadership capacity to align people, resources,
pedagogy, and organizational priorities. Thus, the principal must function as an
orchestrator of transformation rather than simply as an administrator of technological
resources.
        Digital transformation also produces uncertainty that cannot always be addressed
through predetermined procedures. New technologies emerge rapidly, teacher
competencies vary, student expectations change, and educational policies evolve
continuously. Under such circumstances, leadership based exclusively on established
routines may become inadequate.(Yusuf et al., 2025) Adaptive leadership provides a
useful theoretical lens because it emphasizes the capacity to respond to complex

         The COVID-19 pandemic demonstrated the importance of this adaptive capacity.
School principals were suddenly required to shift instructional systems, support teachers
in using digital platforms, communicate with families, and maintain organizational
continuity under unprecedented conditions. Research conducted in Kuwait found that
principals’ digital leadership positively influenced teachers’ technology integration
during the pandemic (AlAjmi, 2022). The study involved 113 principals and 404 teachers
and demonstrated that visionary digital leadership contributed to technology integration.
This evidence is relevant beyond the pandemic because it shows that crisis conditions
accelerate the need for principals who can mobilize technological and human resources
simultaneously.
         The Indonesian educational context presents similar challenges. Digital
transformation is increasingly embedded in curriculum reform, school administration,
teacher professional development, and learning innovation. A systematic literature review
of Indonesian school principals found that digital leadership supports efficient learning
processes and contributes to the achievement of educational objectives (Riski et al.,
2023). The study also emphasizes the importance of digital leadership competencies in
improving educational quality. This finding is particularly significant for Islamic schools
because digital leadership must be contextualized within religious educational objectives
rather than imported as a purely managerial model.
         The concept of digital leadership itself remains multidimensional. It may involve
technological competence, strategic vision, instructional leadership, organizational
change, collaboration, digital communication, and data-based decision-making.
Acebuche (2023), through a systematic review, found that there is no universally agreed
definition of digital leadership because the concept continues to evolve across different
organizational contexts. This conceptual diversity creates a challenge for educational
institutions seeking to develop principal competencies. A principal cannot be considered
digitally competent merely because he or she can use digital applications; digital
leadership must be assessed according to the capacity to create educational value through
technology.
         Digital leadership should consequently be distinguished from simple digital
literacy. Digital literacy refers to an individual’s capacity to understand and use digital
technologies effectively. Digital leadership goes further because it involves influencing
organizational members, establishing strategic direction, allocating resources, managing
change, and building institutional capacity. Håkansson Lindqvist and Pettersson (2019)
found that school leaders understood digitalization as a broad organizational process
rather than simply a technical matter. This distinction is crucial because principal
development programs often emphasize operational technology skills while neglecting
strategic and relational leadership.(Nurhidayah & Handayani, 2025) Therefore, digital
leadership development should prioritize organizational transformation alongside
technological proficiency.

         Islamic educational leadership adds another dimension because leadership is
understood as a moral and spiritual responsibility. The principal is not merely an
institutional manager but an educational role model responsible for cultivating
knowledge, character, faith, and social responsibility. In Islamic educational settings,
leadership should therefore reflect values such as amanah, justice, consultation,
responsibility, integrity, compassion, and uswah ḥasanah. Recent research on Islamic
educational leadership in the digital age similarly emphasizes that technological
innovation should remain connected to Islamic values and educational purposes (Rizki &
Junaidi, 2026). This perspective prevents digital transformation from becoming value-
neutral and places ethics at the center of institutional innovation.
         The relationship between technology and Islamic identity is particularly
important. Digital tools can strengthen Islamic educational identity when they are used to
facilitate Qur’anic learning, Islamic scholarship, character education, communication,
and community engagement. However, technology can also weaken identity when
schools adopt platforms without considering cultural, religious, and ethical implications.
Recent research on Islamic elementary-school management identifies the need for
principals to balance modernization with preservation of Islamic identity. Hamdanah
(2026) identifies visionary technology leadership, professional development, Islamic
integration in digital content, contextualized blended learning, and parental engagement
as major strategies. This evidence demonstrates that digital transformation in Islamic
schools requires contextual adaptation rather than technological imitation.
         Teacher development constitutes another major concern. Principals cannot
implement digital transformation independently because teachers are the primary actors
translating organizational strategy into classroom practice. Research consistently
indicates that teachers’ digital competence, attitudes, confidence, and willingness to
innovate influence the effectiveness of digital transformation. AlAjmi (2022) found that
principal digital leadership had a positive influence on teachers’ technology integration.
Recent systematic reviews likewise emphasize teacher digital competency development
as a central component of effective school digital leadership. These findings mean that
adaptive principals must become developers of people rather than merely controllers of
technology.
         Digital transformation also requires a shift toward collaborative leadership. The
complexity of digitalization means that no principal can possess all the technological,
pedagogical, and organizational knowledge necessary to make every decision. Principals
therefore need to distribute leadership, create professional learning communities, involve
teachers in decision-making, and establish partnerships with parents and external
stakeholders.(Wahidah et al., 2025) Recent literature identifies collaboration and
organizational capacity as important dimensions of digital school leadership. The
explanation is straightforward: digital transformation becomes sustainable when
organizational knowledge is distributed rather than concentrated in a single leader.
         Another critical issue is data-informed decision-making. Digital systems generate
substantial amounts of information about student learning, attendance, teacher
performance, administrative processes, and institutional communication. Principals
increasingly need the ability to interpret data ethically and use it to inform decisions.
Recent reviews of digital leadership identify data literacy as an important competency
alongside digital literacy and information-system management. Data, however, should
not replace professional judgment because numerical indicators may fail to capture

         Digital transformation also raises questions about governance and ethics. Schools
collect student data, use digital platforms, communicate through social media, and
increasingly experiment with artificial intelligence. These practices require clear policies
regarding privacy, cybersecurity, intellectual property, misinformation, algorithmic bias,
and responsible technology use. The principal must therefore understand digital
governance as an ethical responsibility rather than simply a technical function. This
requirement is particularly important in Islamic education because concepts such as
amanah, justice, protection of dignity, and avoidance of harm provide a normative
foundation for responsible digital governance. The result is a model of leadership that
combines digital capability with moral accountability.
         The need for adaptive Islamic educational leadership is therefore not merely
theoretical. Recent research has begun to describe Islamic educational leaders as adaptive,
agile, innovative, and visionary actors responding to digital disruption. Wibowo et al.
(2025) conceptualize agile Islamic educational leadership as a response to changing
learning ecosystems, while Munir and Su’adah (2025) emphasize adaptive, collaborative,
and visionary leadership within Society 5.0. These studies demonstrate the growing
relevance of adaptive leadership in Islamic education. Nevertheless, a systematic
competency framework specifically designed for school principals remains insufficiently
developed.
         This research therefore addresses a significant conceptual and practical gap.
Existing research has separately examined digital leadership, adaptive leadership, Islamic
educational leadership, and school principal competencies. However, fewer studies have
integrated these dimensions into a unified framework that explains what adaptive
competencies principals need and how those competencies relate to Islamic educational
values. Recent reviews also indicate that digital school leadership research remains
fragmented and often focuses more heavily on infrastructure and teacher digital literacy
than on principals’ strategic and pedagogical roles (Asriadi & Andriani, 2026; Nagel et
al., 2026). The present study responds to this gap by developing a conceptual framework
that integrates digital, adaptive, pedagogical, organizational, and Islamic competencies.
         The purpose of this study is therefore to analyze the theoretical and empirical
literature concerning Islamic educational leadership, digital transformation, digital
leadership, adaptive leadership, and principal competencies. The study specifically seeks
to identify the competencies required by school principals to lead Islamic educational
institutions effectively in digitally transforming environments. It also seeks to synthesize
previous research into an integrated framework that is theoretically grounded and
practically applicable. The proposed framework is expected to assist policymakers,
principal preparation programs, Islamic educational institutions, and researchers in
understanding the competencies necessary for sustainable digital transformation.
Ultimately, the study argues that effective digital transformation depends less on the
quantity of technology available than on the quality of leadership that gives technology
educational, organizational, and ethical direction.

         Islamic educational leadership is fundamentally concerned with guiding
educational institutions toward educational, moral, spiritual, and social purposes.
Leadership in this context cannot be separated from the concepts of amanah, justice,
consultation, responsibility, and exemplary conduct. The principal is simultaneously an
organizational leader and an educational model whose behavior communicates
institutional values. Contemporary Islamic educational leadership research increasingly
emphasizes the need to preserve these normative foundations while responding to
technological and social change (Rizki & Junaidi, 2026). This theoretical position means
that digital transformation must remain subordinate to educational and ethical purposes
rather than becoming an end in itself.
         The concept of amanah is especially relevant to principal leadership in the digital
era. Digital systems give principals access to extensive institutional and student
information, making confidentiality and responsible information management essential.
A principal who treats digital data carelessly can undermine trust even if the school
appears technologically advanced.(Kashada et al., 2025) Islamic leadership therefore
requires the integration of technological competence with ethical accountability. The
contemporary literature on digital governance reinforces this need by emphasizing data
responsibility, cybersecurity, and institutional trust as components of digital
transformation. Thus, amanah can serve as an Islamic ethical foundation for digital
governance.
         Justice is another essential dimension. Digital transformation can create unequal
opportunities when some teachers have greater technological competence or when some
students have superior access to devices and connectivity. Principals must therefore
identify and address digital inequalities rather than assume that technology automatically
produces educational equality.(Nurdi et al., 2025) Research on digital school leadership
indicates that organizational capacity and access conditions significantly influence digital
transformation outcomes. From an Islamic perspective, justice requires principals to
allocate resources fairly and protect vulnerable members of the school community.
         Consultation or shūrā provides a foundation for collaborative digital leadership.
Technology implementation frequently affects teachers’ workload, teaching methods,
assessment practices, and professional autonomy. Decisions imposed without
consultation may produce resistance even when the technology itself is beneficial. Recent
studies of digital leadership emphasize collaboration, professional learning communities,
and teacher involvement in transformation. Therefore, shūrā can be operationalized as
participatory decision-making in digital transformation.
         The principle of uswah ḥasanah also has direct relevance. Teachers are more
likely to embrace digital innovation when principals demonstrate openness to learning,
willingness to experiment, and readiness to acknowledge mistakes. A principal who
demands digital competence from teachers but refuses to learn new technologies sends a
contradictory message. Digital transformation therefore requires leadership by example.
This principle is consistent with research showing that leaders’ practices and attitudes
shape the conditions under which teachers adopt technology.

technologies to achieve organizational and educational goals. It encompasses more than
the technical ability to operate digital platforms. Digital leaders establish vision, develop
organizational capacity, facilitate innovation, manage change, and create conditions for

digital leadership and notes that no single universally accepted definition has yet emerged.
This conceptual diversity indicates that digital leadership should be interpreted
contextually according to institutional purposes and organizational needs.
         The strategic dimension of digital leadership is particularly important for
principals. Technology purchases without a strategic rationale can produce fragmented
systems, unused platforms, and unnecessary financial burdens. Håkansson Lindqvist and
Pettersson (2019) demonstrate that digitalization creates complex organizational
challenges that require leaders to prioritize resources and professional development. The
principal must therefore develop a digital transformation roadmap connected to the
school’s educational mission. In Islamic education, that roadmap must also include the
institution’s religious and moral objectives.
         Instructional digital leadership represents another dimension. Principals need to
understand how technology can improve teaching, assessment, student engagement, and
learning outcomes. Digital leadership becomes ineffective when technology is
concentrated in administrative systems while classroom pedagogy remains unchanged.
AlAjmi (2022) demonstrates that principal digital leadership can positively influence
teachers’ technology integration. This finding indicates that principals should connect
technology decisions directly to instructional improvement.
         Digital leadership also involves organizational culture. Schools need cultures in
which experimentation, learning, collaboration, and responsible risk-taking are accepted.
A rigid culture can transform digital innovation into compliance rather than meaningful
improvement. Recent systematic reviews identify digital culture and professional learning
communities as important strategies for successful digital leadership. Thus, principals
should develop environments where teachers can experiment, share practices, evaluate
failures, and improve collectively.
Adaptive Leadership
         Adaptive leadership focuses on how leaders mobilize individuals and
organizations to address complex challenges that cannot be solved through existing
technical solutions. Digital transformation contains both technical and adaptive
dimensions. Installing a learning management system may be a technical problem, but
changing teachers’ beliefs about teaching and assessment is an adaptive
challenge.(Barton et al., 2021) Similarly, purchasing artificial intelligence tools is
technical, whereas determining their ethical and pedagogical implications is adaptive.
This distinction is important because principals must know when to apply technical
expertise and when to facilitate organizational learning.
         Adaptive leadership requires leaders to tolerate uncertainty. Digital
transformation rarely follows a perfectly predictable sequence because technological
possibilities, stakeholder responses, and institutional conditions continually change.
Principals must therefore monitor emerging conditions and revise strategies rather than
rigidly implement predetermined plans. Recent Islamic educational leadership literature
describes adaptive and agile leadership as necessary responses to disruption in learning
ecosystems (Wibowo et al., 2025). This finding supports the integration of adaptive
capacity into principal competency frameworks.
         Adaptive leadership also requires distributed problem-solving. Complex
organizational problems cannot always be solved by senior leaders alone because relevant
knowledge is distributed among teachers, students, technicians, parents, and community

         Another adaptive competency is the ability to manage resistance. Resistance

concerns. Recent research indicates that inclusive digital leadership can reduce teacher
resistance while improving digital competence.
Transformational and Instructional Leadership
         Transformational leadership contributes to digital transformation by emphasizing
vision, motivation, empowerment, and organizational change. A principal needs to
communicate why digital transformation matters and how it contributes to the school’s
mission. Technology becomes meaningful when teachers can connect it with student
learning, institutional improvement, and educational values.(Rifai et al., 2025) Recent
studies of digital transformational leadership identify organizational agility and teacher
readiness as important mechanisms through which digital leadership affects school
development.
         Instructional leadership complements transformational leadership by keeping
student learning at the center. A school can become highly digitalized without becoming
educationally effective. Therefore, principals need to evaluate whether technology
improves instructional quality, student engagement, assessment, inclusion, and learning
outcomes. Håkansson Lindqvist (2019) emphasizes that school leaders play an important
role in creating conditions for innovative technology-enhanced teaching and learning.
The theoretical implication is that digital transformation should always be evaluated
according to pedagogical value.
         The integration of transformational and instructional leadership creates a balanced
model. Transformational leadership provides direction and motivation, while
instructional leadership ensures that transformation remains connected to learning.
Adaptive leadership adds the capacity to adjust strategies under uncertainty. Islamic
educational leadership contributes moral and spiritual orientation.(Ali et al., 2025) Digital
leadership provides technological and organizational mechanisms. The proposed
framework therefore does not replace existing leadership theories but integrates them into
a contextually appropriate model.
Previous Research
         Previous empirical research provides strong evidence that principal digital
leadership affects teachers’ technology use. AlAjmi (2022) found a positive effect of
principals’ digital leadership on technology integration among teachers in Kuwait. The
study is significant because it empirically connects principal behavior with classroom
technology adoption. It also demonstrates that digital leadership becomes particularly
important during periods of disruption. The study therefore supports the argument that
digital competence should be regarded as a core principal competency rather than an
optional skill.

        Håkansson Lindqvist (2019) examined school leaders’ practices in a three-year
digital initiative and found that leaders created conditions supporting new methods,
sharing, and collaboration. At the same time, they faced technical difficulties, teacher-
support demands, and limitations of time. This research illustrates the complexity of
digital leadership in everyday practice. It also suggests that principals require
organizational support rather than being expected to lead digitalization individually.
        Håkansson Lindqvist and Pettersson (2019) further found that school leaders
understand digitalization as a multidimensional process involving technical, pedagogical,
administrative, and organizational issues. This study is theoretically important because it
challenges narrow definitions of digital leadership. The principal must navigate several
systems simultaneously. Consequently, principal competency frameworks should include
technological, pedagogical, organizational, and strategic dimensions.
        Riski et al. (2023) reviewed digital leadership among Indonesian school principals
and concluded that digital leadership supports efficient learning and educational goal
achievement. Their study is particularly relevant to the Indonesian context. It
demonstrates that digital leadership is not simply an international trend but an
increasingly important issue within Indonesian school management. Nevertheless, the
study does not specifically integrate Islamic leadership values or adaptive leadership
theory.
        Asriadi and Andriani (2026) synthesized 42 empirical studies and found that
existing research emphasizes infrastructure readiness and teacher digital literacy more
than principals’ strategic roles and pedagogical leadership. They also found that
organizational and relational effects are more consistently reported than direct learning
effects. This reveals an important research gap. Digital leadership research needs to
examine not only whether schools become digitally ready but also how principals create
organizational conditions for meaningful pedagogical transformation.
        Nagel et al. (2026) similarly found that research on digitalization and school
leadership remains fragmented and theoretically underdeveloped. Their scoping review
identified limited longitudinal and mixed-method research and emphasized the need to
understand how digital leadership is enacted in organizational, technological, and
governance contexts. This finding reinforces the importance of conceptual frameworks
that move beyond individualized views of leadership. It also supports the present study’s
emphasis on contextual and systemic competencies.
        Recent Islamic education studies have begun addressing this gap. Wibowo et al.
(2025) propose agile Islamic educational leadership as a response to learning ecosystem
disruption, while Rizki and Junaidi (2026) identify adaptive, innovative, and visionary
characteristics as important for Islamic educational leaders. Hamdanah (2026)
emphasizes the integration of technology leadership with Islamic identity, professional
development, blended learning, and parental engagement. These studies provide valuable
foundations but do not yet establish a comprehensive competency matrix for school
principals. The present research therefore synthesizes these strands into an eight-
competency framework.

conceptual framework rather than test a statistical hypothesis through field data. The

approximately 2019 and 2026. This period was selected because it captures the
accelerated development of digital leadership research, including the transformation
produced by the COVID-19 pandemic and subsequent developments in artificial
intelligence. Earlier theoretical works were considered only when they remained
necessary for conceptual clarification. Priority was given to peer-reviewed research
directly addressing educational leadership, digitalization, school principals, Islamic
education, and organizational transformation. This strategy ensures that the conceptual
framework is anchored in recent educational realities.
        The literature search used combinations of keywords including “Islamic
educational leadership,” “digital transformation and school principals,” “digital
leadership,” “adaptive leadership in education,” “Islamic school leadership,” “digital
leadership competencies,” and “educational leadership in the digital era.” Searches were
conducted through scholarly databases, publisher platforms, institutional repositories, and
academic indexing services. The search process also examined reference lists of relevant
articles to identify additional studies. Recent systematic reviews were particularly useful
for identifying major themes and recurring findings.
        The inclusion criteria consisted of peer-reviewed journal articles published
primarily within the most recent five to ten years, studies directly relevant to educational
leadership or digital transformation, and articles containing conceptual, empirical,
systematic-review, or qualitative evidence. Studies focused exclusively on business
leadership were excluded unless their theoretical contribution was directly transferable to
educational leadership. Articles with insufficient methodological or conceptual
information were also excluded. This procedure strengthened the relevance and reliability
of the literature synthesis.
        Data were analyzed using thematic content analysis. The first analytical category
concerned the changing role of school principals in digital transformation. The second
category addressed digital leadership competencies, while the third examined adaptive
leadership characteristics. The fourth category focused on Islamic leadership values and
the fifth examined teacher development, organizational culture, innovation, and
governance. The categories were subsequently compared to identify areas of convergence
and gaps.
        The synthesis was conducted through comparison and conceptual integration.
Findings from digital leadership research were compared with findings from Islamic
educational leadership and adaptive leadership research. Similar competencies were
consolidated, while distinct competencies were preserved when they represented
meaningful theoretical differences. For example, digital literacy was distinguished from
strategic digital vision, and technological expertise was distinguished from adaptive
change management. This distinction is important because effective principals require
both knowledge of technology and the leadership capacity to mobilize people around
technological change.

validated, so future studies should test its reliability and applicability in different types of
Islamic schools. The methodology nevertheless provides a systematic basis for generating
theoretically informed propositions from contemporary scholarship.

Results and Discussion
Findings from the Literature Synthesis
         The literature synthesis reveals that digital transformation changes the principal’s
role in at least five major ways. First, principals become strategic technology leaders
rather than technology users alone. Second, they become facilitators of organizational
learning and teacher development. Third, they become instructional leaders responsible
for ensuring that digital tools improve pedagogy. Fourth, they become ethical governors
of data and digital environments. Fifth, they become adaptive change leaders capable of
navigating uncertainty and resistance. These dimensions are consistent with the findings
of recent systematic reviews of digital school leadership.
         The second major finding is that digital leadership cannot be separated from
organizational culture. Technology adoption depends on whether teachers perceive
innovation as meaningful, manageable, and supported by leadership. Principals therefore
need to establish cultures of experimentation, collaboration, reflection, and professional
learning. Håkansson Lindqvist (2019) found that school leaders’ support for collaboration
and new ways of working contributed to technology-enhanced learning environments.
This indicates that culture is a central mechanism connecting leadership behavior with
digital transformation.
         The third finding concerns the importance of adaptive capacity. Digital
transformation involves problems that are simultaneously technological, pedagogical,
cultural, and ethical. Technical solutions alone cannot resolve teacher anxiety,
organizational resistance, or conflicts over educational values. Principals must therefore
diagnose the type of challenge, determine which aspects require technical intervention,
and identify which require behavioral or cultural adaptation. This finding supports the
integration of adaptive leadership into digital leadership frameworks.
         The fourth finding is that teacher competence is one of the most important
mediating factors. Principals can establish sophisticated digital strategies, but
implementation depends on teachers’ ability and willingness to translate them into
instructional practice. AlAjmi (2022) provides empirical evidence that principal digital
leadership positively influences teacher technology integration. Recent systematic
reviews similarly identify teacher digital competence as a central component of digital
transformation. The implication is that principal competency must include the ability to
design and sustain teacher professional learning.
         The fifth finding is that Islamic educational leadership must preserve normative
orientation. Technology should support the school’s educational and spiritual mission
rather than displace it. Recent Islamic education research emphasizes adaptive,

a single project but an ongoing process of experimentation, evaluation, scaling, and
renewal. Principals must determine which innovations should be adopted, adapted, tested,
expanded, or discontinued. This requires strategic prioritization rather than enthusiasm
for every new technological trend. The literature on digital transformational leadership
similarly emphasizes organizational agility as a mechanism for responding to changing
technological conditions.
         The tenth finding concerns ethical governance. Artificial intelligence, digital
communication, online learning platforms, and student information systems create new
ethical responsibilities. Principals must establish policies for privacy, cybersecurity,
responsible AI, academic integrity, digital citizenship, and appropriate communication.
Islamic ethical principles such as amanah, justice, protection from harm, and respect for
human dignity can provide a normative foundation for these policies. Thus, ethical digital
governance is not an additional competency but a central component of Islamic
educational leadership.

educational leadership, as it provides the strategic direction through which technological
transformation is aligned with institutional purposes. A digitally adaptive principal needs
to articulate a compelling institutional vision explaining how technology supports
learning, character formation, administration, and community engagement. Such a vision
should be systematically connected to the school’s Islamic identity and strategic
objectives rather than being framed merely as an effort to modernize technological
infrastructure. Research on digital leadership consistently indicates that visionary
leadership enables educational institutions to optimize technological resources and
integrate digital technologies into meaningful educational practices (AlAjmi, 2022).

         Closely associated with the capacity for strategic vision is adaptive intelligence,
which enables school principals to respond constructively to dynamic and uncertain
environments. Adaptive intelligence refers to the ability to recognize changing
conditions, interpret complexity, learn from experience, and modify leadership strategies
accordingly. This competency is particularly significant in the context of digital
transformation because technologies, educational policies, and stakeholder expectations
continue to evolve at a rapid pace. An adaptive principal, therefore, does not regard an
initial transformation plan as a fixed blueprint but continuously utilizes feedback,
emerging evidence, and contextual developments to refine institutional strategies. Recent
scholarship on Islamic educational leadership likewise emphasizes agile and adaptive
approaches as increasingly relevant mechanisms for responding to the disruption and
complexity generated by digital transformation.
         Beyond strategic adaptability, effective digital transformation requires principals
to demonstrate pedagogical-digital leadership that integrates technological knowledge
with the core purposes of teaching and learning. Principals need to understand how
technology can enhance instructional design, assessment, collaboration, personalization,
and student engagement. Although school leaders are not necessarily required to possess
advanced programming expertise, they need adequate pedagogical and technological
literacy to critically evaluate digital innovations and determine their educational
relevance. Håkansson Lindqvist (2019) demonstrates that school leaders play a crucial
role in creating organizational conditions that facilitate innovative digital teaching
practices. From this perspective, technology acquires educational value not through its
mere adoption, but through leadership capacity to connect technological innovation with
authentic pedagogical processes and intended learning outcomes.
         The sustainability of digital transformation is further contingent upon the
principal’s capacity to empower teachers through collaborative leadership structures.
Digital transformation requires teachers to function as active agents of institutional
change rather than passive recipients of externally formulated policies. Accordingly,
principals should develop professional learning communities, mentoring systems,
innovation teams, and participatory decision-making mechanisms that facilitate collective
learning and shared responsibility. Recent research on digital leadership in Indonesia
identifies teacher competency development and collaborative organizational culture as
important determinants of successful technology implementation. This competency also
corresponds closely with the Islamic principle of shūrā, through which meaningful
consultation facilitates the distribution of responsibility, collective deliberation, and the
utilization of institutional knowledge in decision-making processes.
         Another critical dimension of digitally adaptive leadership is the capacity to
employ data as an informed basis for institutional decision-making. Principals should
possess sufficient data literacy to interpret digital information, identify institutional
problems, evaluate educational programs, monitor implementation, and determine
appropriate resource allocation. Nevertheless, data should be regarded as empirical
evidence that informs judgment rather than as an unquestionable source of truth.
Educational decision-making must therefore integrate quantitative evidence with
qualitative information, professional judgment, student experiences, teacher perspectives,
and contextual considerations. Recent scholarship increasingly identifies data literacy as

         The successful institutionalization of technological innovation also depends upon
the principal’s competence in innovation leadership and change management. Principals
need to understand how to introduce innovations incrementally, communicate their

implementation outcomes, and scale practices that demonstrate meaningful educational

or contextual concerns rather than from an inherent rejection of innovation. Recent

creates an imperative for ethically grounded digital governance. Islamic school principals
must establish clear institutional policies concerning data privacy, cybersecurity,
responsible artificial intelligence, online communication, academic integrity, and digital
citizenship. This competency represents a distinctive dimension of Islamic digital
leadership because it explicitly connects technological governance with moral and ethical
principles derived from Islamic educational thought. The principle of amanah requires
leaders to exercise responsible stewardship over institutional and personal information,
while the principle of justice (‘adl) demands equitable access, fair treatment, and non-
discriminatory implementation of digital systems. Furthermore, the principle of avoiding
harm requires principals to critically anticipate the psychological, social, educational, and
security consequences associated with the adoption and use of digital technologies.
Finally, the long-term sustainability of adaptive digital leadership depends upon a culture
of continuous professional learning among both principals and teachers.
         Digital transformation cannot be sustained when professional development is
limited to initial training because technological systems, pedagogical approaches, and
institutional demands continue to evolve. Professional learning must consequently be
understood as an ongoing organizational process through which educational leaders and
teachers continually update their knowledge, competencies, and practices. Håkansson
Lindqvist (2019) emphasizes the importance of organizational support and sustained
professional development for educational leaders operating within increasingly
digitalized environments. This orientation is also compatible with the Islamic educational
tradition, which places a high value on the continuous pursuit of knowledge. Accordingly,
lifelong professional learning should be positioned not merely as a technical requirement
of digital transformation but as an integral component of an Islamic educational
leadership philosophy that emphasizes intellectual growth, professional responsibility,
and continuous improvement.The framework also indicates that these eight competencies

leadership protects the educational purpose, collaboration mobilizes people, data informs
decisions, innovation management structures transformation, ethical governance protects
institutional values, and professional learning sustains the entire system. The

        The framework has implications for principal recruitment and preparation.
Traditional principal selection often emphasizes administrative experience, managerial
knowledge, and general leadership ability. In a digitally transforming environment, these
competencies remain necessary but are no longer sufficient. Principal preparation should
include digital strategy, AI literacy, data governance, digital pedagogy, organizational
change, cybersecurity awareness, and ethical decision-making. For Islamic schools, these
areas should be integrated with Islamic leadership values rather than taught as unrelated
technical modules.
        The framework also has implications for professional development. Training
should not consist exclusively of workshops demonstrating how to operate applications.
Instead, principals should engage in strategic simulations, case analysis, peer coaching,
action research, innovation projects, and reflective leadership activities. Such approaches
develop adaptive competence because principals learn to diagnose problems and make
decisions under uncertainty. Recent research on digital leadership similarly indicates that
professional learning is central to sustainable digital transformation.
        At the organizational level, principals need to create digital transformation teams.
These teams can include teachers, administrative staff, technology specialists, student
representatives, and parent representatives. The team can conduct digital readiness
assessments, identify priorities, monitor implementation, and evaluate outcomes. Such
structures reduce the risk that digital transformation becomes dependent on a single
principal. They also create organizational resilience when leadership changes.
        Another implication concerns resource management. Digital transformation
requires investment in infrastructure, software, connectivity, professional development,
technical support, and cybersecurity. However, resource allocation should be guided by
educational priorities rather than technological enthusiasm. Principals should evaluate
whether each investment contributes to learning, equity, institutional efficiency, or
character development. The Islamic principle of responsible stewardship provides an
ethical basis for avoiding waste and prioritizing genuine educational benefit.
        The framework also emphasizes the importance of digital inclusion. Schools
should identify students and teachers who face barriers to access, including inadequate
devices, weak connectivity, disability-related barriers, and limited digital competence.
Digital transformation should not create a new form of educational inequality. A principal
guided by justice must ensure that technological innovation expands opportunities rather
than privileges already advantaged groups. This dimension is particularly important in
diverse Indonesian educational contexts.
        The role of parents is equally important. Digital transformation changes the
relationship between school and home because students frequently learn, communicate,
and consume information through digital environments outside school. Islamic school
principals therefore need to establish parent education programs concerning digital
parenting, online safety, responsible technology use, and Islamic digital ethics. Recent
research on Islamic elementary education identifies parental engagement as one of the
strategic dimensions of digital transformation.

be regarded merely as recipients of digital transformation. They can become partners in
developing digital citizenship, peer mentoring, media literacy, and technology-based
social projects. Principals can establish student digital councils or innovation teams that
provide feedback about technology use. This approach strengthens student agency and
helps leaders understand how digital transformation is experienced by learners.
        Artificial intelligence represents an emerging challenge for adaptive principals.
AI can support personalized learning, administrative efficiency, content creation,
assessment, and professional development. At the same time, it raises concerns about
academic integrity, hallucinated information, privacy, bias, dependence, and the erosion
of human judgment. The principal must therefore create an institutional AI policy
grounded in educational objectives and Islamic ethics. The appropriate leadership
response is neither unconditional adoption nor blanket rejection but informed, ethical, and
pedagogically justified experimentation.

through the existence and implementation of a strategic roadmap. Adaptive intelligence
can be assessed through responsiveness to emerging problems and evidence of iterative
planning. Pedagogical leadership can be assessed through changes in teaching practices
and learning quality. Collaborative empowerment can be measured through participation
and professional learning structures. Data governance can be assessed through policy
quality and responsible use of information. Finally, the framework demonstrates that the
ultimate objective of digital transformation should be educational improvement rather
than digitalization itself. A school should not be considered successful merely because it
has a sophisticated learning management system, electronic administration, or artificial
intelligence tools. Success should be judged according to whether technology strengthens
learning, teacher professionalism, student character, inclusion, institutional effectiveness,
and Islamic educational identity. This conclusion is consistent with recent reviews
showing that the effects of digital leadership are more consistently observed when
organizational and relational conditions are addressed rather than when infrastructure is
treated as the central outcome.

Conclusion
         Digital transformation has fundamentally expanded the responsibilities of Islamic
school principals. The principal is no longer adequately described as an administrator who
manages existing organizational routines but must increasingly function as a digital
strategist, instructional leader, change agent, organizational learner, ethical governor, and
developer of people. The literature demonstrates that successful digital transformation
depends on the interaction between technological readiness, teacher competence,
organizational culture, strategic vision, and leadership capacity. Within Islamic
educational institutions, these dimensions must remain connected to amanah, justice,
shūrā, uswah ḥasanah, iṣlāḥ, and the pursuit of beneficial knowledge. The study therefore
proposes eight adaptive Islamic digital leadership competencies: digital vision, adaptive
intelligence, pedagogical-digital leadership, collaborative empowerment, data-informed
decision-making, innovation and change management, ethical-digital governance, and
continuous professional learning. The novelty of the study lies in integrating digital
leadership, adaptive leadership, and Islamic educational leadership into a single
competency framework specifically oriented toward school principals. The framework
conceptualizes digital transformation as an organizational, pedagogical, cultural, and
ethical process rather than as technological adoption alone. Future empirical research
should validate the eight competencies through quantitative instruments, qualitative case
studies, and longitudinal investigations across madrasahs, Islamic schools, and pesantren-
based educational institutions. Comparative research across Indonesia and other Muslim-
majority contexts would also be valuable for examining how local cultures and
institutional traditions shape adaptive Islamic digital leadership. Ultimately, the
effectiveness of digital transformation in Islamic education will depend not on how
rapidly schools acquire new technologies but on whether their leaders can transform
technological change into meaningful educational improvement while preserving Islamic
ethical and spiritual foundations.
